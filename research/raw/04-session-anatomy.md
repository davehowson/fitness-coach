# Session Anatomy: Building a Single Workout

> Research agent output · Topic 04 · 2026-10-08

## Executive summary

- A session = **warm-up → (optional power) → main compound lift(s) → secondary compounds → accessories/isolation → core → conditioning → optional short cool-down**. Order rule: put the highest-priority, most technical/fatiguing exercise first; ACSM says large-before-small, multi-joint-before-single-joint, high-intensity-before-low [S14]. Evidence: exercise placed first gains more strength; hypertrophy is largely order-independent over short trials [S5]. (Strong for strength / Moderate for hypertrophy)
- **Warm-up**: 5–10 min general + specific ramp sets of the first lift. Static stretching as the *sole* warm-up slightly reduces strength/power; short holds (≤45–60 s) inside a full warm-up are harmless; dynamic work is neutral-to-positive [S2][S3][S1]. Moderate.
- **Specific warm-up sets** (light → near working load) are the most practical, best-supported element for lifting; the highest-supported detail is that sets near working load help maximal strength, but the evidence is mixed [S16][S17]. Moderate/Practitioner.
- **Per-session volume**: direct evidence shows diminishing returns within a session, but data are thin. A meta-regression suggests the per-session "point of diminishing returns" ≈ 2 direct sets (strength) and ≈ 11 fractional sets (hypertrophy) per muscle [S6] (preprint, Moderate-low). Practitioner default: ≤ ~6–10 hard direct sets/muscle/session; split more volume over more days. Weekly volume matters more than distribution [S8][S9].
- **Exercise count**: no research-derived cap; practitioner range 4–8 exercises per 45–75 min session. Minimal-effective-dose evidence: ≥4 weekly sets per muscle at 6–15 RM yields gains [S8].
- **Rest**: >60 s gives a small hypertrophy edge over very short rest; no appreciable gain beyond ~90 s for hypertrophy [S12]. ACSM suggests 3–5 min for heavy (1–6RM) lifts [S14]. Rest for compounds longer than for isolation is practitioner norm.
- **Supersets**: antagonist pairings cut session time ~up to half with no loss in hypertrophy/strength outcomes; costs are higher perceived exertion and muscle damage [S7][S8]. Same-muscle pairings cut volume load [S7].
- **Cool-down**: stretching afterward doesn't clinically reduce DOMS or speed strength recovery; evidence quality very low [S10][S11]. A 3–5 min easy cool-down is optional/preference.
- **Balance**: push:pull ≈ 1:1 (or pulling slightly higher) is a practitioner convention; no trial shows that ratio prevents injury [S20]. Unilateral vs bilateral: equal hypertrophy; strength follows specificity [S19].

## Core concepts

**General → specific.** A session has a goal (strength, hypertrophy, power, conditioning) and a time budget. Quality work (heavy, technical, high-velocity) must happen while fresh; fatigue-tolerant work goes later.

**Block definitions**
- *Warm-up*: raise temperature/HR, mobilise joints, activate target muscle, rehearse pattern. The RAMP framework (Jeffreys) organises it as **R**aise → **A**ctivate & **M**obilise → **P**otentiate (ramp toward session intensity) [S4]. RAMP is a practitioner framework, not a tested protocol. (Practitioner)
- *Power block*: jumps, throws, Olympic-lift derivatives; low reps, full recovery; first because fatigue degrades velocity/technique.
- *Main lift*: squat/hinge/press/pull variation, heaviest loading, longest rests.
- *Secondary compound*: a second pattern or variation, moderate load, 6–12 reps.
- *Accessory / isolation*: single-joint or machine work, 8–20 reps, close to failure; targets lagging muscles.
- *Core*: anti-extension/rotation/lateral flexion; can be placed early as a superset filler or late.
- *Conditioning/finisher*: last so it does not impair lifting (interference is covered in another topic).
- *Cool-down*: optional.

**Why order matters (mechanism).** Fatigue accumulates, so earlier exercises get greater loads and reps. Specificity of strength follows: exercises done first show larger strength gains [S5]. Hypertrophy depends on total hard sets, so order is less critical.

**Diminishing returns per session.** Within-session, each additional set adds a smaller stimulus; counting method matters (direct sets vs fractional sets that credit synergist work) [S6].

**Density techniques.** Supersets pair two exercises with minimal rest between; antagonist (agonist–antagonist), agonist (same muscle), and non-competing (unrelated muscles) varieties; tri-sets (3), giant sets (4+), circuits (stations rotated) [S7][S8].

## Key findings

1. **Warm-ups generally improve performance.** A systematic review with meta-analysis (Fradkin 2010) found warm-up had a positive effect in the large majority (~80%) of studies [S1] (secondary figure, via a coaching summary). **Strong-ish / Moderate** (mostly non-lifting performance tasks, mostly temperature-related).
2. **Static stretching alone before max effort slightly hurts strength/power.** Meta-analysis: pooled strength effect about −0.10 SD; larger on isometric tests; smallest with holds ≤45 s; conclusion: avoid SS as the sole warm-up [S2]. Behm 2016 review: mean change about −3.7% for SS, +1.3% dynamic, −4.4% PNF immediately after stretching; inside a full warm-up with a sport-specific component, SS (esp. ≤60 s) causes no meaningful impairment [S3]. **Strong** that long SS-only is harmful acutely; **Moderate** that short SS in full warm-up is neutral.
3. **Specific warm-up sets: mixed evidence.** A systematic review of 11 studies found no consensus on general + specific warm-up, but maximal strength and reps can be improved when the specific warm-up uses loads close to the maximum [S16]. A study of 40 trained men found 6 sets at 80% of working load gave higher mean propulsive velocity in squat than 6 sets at 40% (small–moderate effect) [S17]. **Moderate-low.** Practitioner prescription: 4–6 dynamic drills + 1–2 light sets of 4–8 reps of the main lift [S18]. **Practitioner.**
4. **Exercise order and strength.** Meta-analysis of 11 studies: greater strength gains for exercises placed at the start of the session [S5]. **Moderate** (short studies, ≤12 wk).
5. **Exercise order and hypertrophy.** Similar hypertrophy regardless of order (g≈0.03, CI −0.26–0.31) [S5]. **Moderate.**
6. **ACSM order guidance**: large before small muscle groups, multi-joint before single-joint, higher before lower intensity; rest 3–5 min for heavy 1–6RM work for intermediate/advanced lifters; ≤90 s for endurance-oriented 15–20 rep work; frequency 2–3 d/wk novice, 3–4 intermediate, 4–5 advanced [S14]. A 2026 ACSM position stand update exists (overview of reviews, 137+ studies); check it for current numbers [S15]. **Strong** (position stand; 2009 version criticised for methodological rigour).
7. **Weekly volume dose-response.** Meta-regression: each added weekly set → ~0.37% more growth; growth increases up to 10+ sets/muscle/week; little data beyond that; mostly untrained subjects [S9]. **Moderate.**
8. **Per-session volume.** Meta-regression (preprint, not peer reviewed): diminishing returns beyond ~2 direct sets/session for strength and ~11 fractional sets for hypertrophy; insufficient data at very high per-session volumes [S6]. **Moderate-low / Contested.**
9. **Minimal effective dose.** Narrative review: ≥4 weekly sets per muscle at 6–15RM; prioritise bilateral multi-joint lifts (a leg press/squat, an upper-body pull, an upper-body push); weekly volume matters more than frequency; advanced techniques (supersets, drop sets, rest-pause) roughly halve time while maintaining volume; probably better for hypertrophy than strength [S8]. Critics: MED should not be assumed for athletes [S8 note]. **Moderate.**
10. **Supersets (systematic review + meta-analysis, 19 studies, n=313).** Agonist–antagonist supersets increased reps completed (SMD 0.68); same-biomechanics supersets reduced volume load (SMD −1.08); hypertrophy no difference (SMD −0.05); shorter sessions without losing strength/hypertrophy; higher perceived exertion and muscle damage [S7]. **Moderate** (short-term studies).
11. **Rest intervals.** Bayesian meta-analysis: small hypertrophy benefit when resting >60 s, probably through higher volume load; no appreciable difference >90 s [S12]. **Moderate.** (Also an 8-wk RCT, 1 vs 3 min rest, showed squat 1RM favoured 3 min; commentary—not independently verified here.)
12. **Cool-down/stretching.** Cochrane review: stretching before/after exercise doesn't produce clinically important DOMS reduction (~1 point on 100-pt scale at 24 h) [S10]. Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (n=229): no effect of post-exercise stretching on DOMS at 24/48/72 h or strength recovery; very low confidence, ~70% high risk of bias [S11]. **Moderate** for "no clear benefit"; **no evidence of harm either**.
13. **Unilateral vs bilateral.** 2025 meta-analysis (9 studies): no significant hypertrophy difference; bilateral training improves bilateral strength more, unilateral improves unilateral strength (specificity); wide CIs [S19]. **Moderate-low.**
14. **Push:pull.** Not found as a trial-tested ratio. Occupational research links pushing/pulling exposure with shoulder symptoms, but this does not translate to gym ratios [S20]. The common coaching rule of ≥1:1 pull:push is **Practitioner**. Not findable: direct trials of push:pull ratio for injury prevention.
15. **Pattern coverage (knee vs hip dominant, carries, anti-rotation).** No controlled research found on weekly balance; treat as **Practitioner** convention (see Related topics for exercise selection).

## Numeric guidelines

### Warm-up

| Component | Dose | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| General (Raise) | 3–10 min easy cardio/dynamic movement, until warm / light sweat | Practitioner [S4][S18] |
| Mobilise/activate | 4–6 dynamic drills, 1–2 sets of 5–10 reps | Practitioner [S18] |
| Specific ramp sets, first lift | 2–4 sets, e.g. 40–50% × 5, 60–70% × 3–5, 80–85% × 1–3 of working weight (heavy lifts); 1 set at ~50% × 8 (accessory-only start) | Practitioner/Moderate [S16][S17] |
| Static stretch (if used) | ≤30–45 s per muscle, ≤60 s max, only inside a full warm-up | Moderate [S2][S3] |
| Total | 5 min (30-min session) to 10–15 min (heavy session) | Practitioner |

| Block | Reps | Sets | Rest | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power (jumps/throws) | 1–5 | 3–5 | 1–3 min | maximal velocity, stop before slowdown |

### Per-session capacity (practitioner defaults informed by [S6][S8][S9])

| Quantity | Default range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hard direct sets per muscle per session | 3–8 (cap ~10) | Beyond ~6–8, redistribute to another day |

### Time budgets

Assume ~2.5–4.5 min per straight set including rest for compounds, ~1.5–2.5 min for isolation. Supersets cut this by about 30–50% [S7][S8].

| Session | Warm-up | Main block | Accessory | Core/Cond. | Total sets | Exercises |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 min | 4–5 | 1 compound × 3 sets (12) | 2–3 exercises supersetted (10) | 2–3 min | 10–14 | 3–5 |
| 45 min | 6–7 | 1–2 compounds × 3 (18) | 2–3 supersetted (15) | 4 | 14–18 | 4–6 |
| 60 min | 8–10 | main 3–4 + secondary 3 (25) | 3 exercises (18) | 5 | 18–22 | 5–7 |
| 75 min | 10 | main 4–5 + secondary 3–4 (30) | 4 exercises (20) | 10 | 22–28 | 6–8 |
| 90 min | 10–12 | main 5 + secondary 4 (35) | 4–5 exercises (25) | 10–12 | 26–34 | 7–9 |

(Time budgets are Practitioner estimates, derived arithmetic.)

### What to cut first when short on time (order)

1. Cool-down and stretching [S10][S11].
2. Conditioning finisher and extra core.
3. Third+ isolation exercise and redundant accessories.
4. Sets 4+ of any exercise (keep ≥2 hard sets).
5. Lengthen nothing; instead superset antagonist/non-competing accessories [S7][S8].
6. Last: reduce main lift to 2–3 sets and ramp warm-up to 2 sets. Keep the main compound; per [S8] keep one leg, one push, one pull multi-joint exercise.

### Pairing strategies

| Type | Example | When | Time saving | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antagonist superset | Bench + row; curl + triceps pushdown | Default time-saver for accessories and even moderate compounds | ~30–50% [S7][S8] | Higher RPE |
| Non-competing (upper/lower) | Press + split squat | Compounds with low interference | ~30–40% | Cardiovascular load |
| Agonist (same muscle) | Incline press + flyes | Hypertrophy finisher (pre-exhaust/post-exhaust) | Moderate | Reduces volume load [S7]; use on isolation only |
| Tri-set/giant set | 3–4 exercises, 1 muscle or 1 region | Pump finisher, short sessions | Large | Fatigue, quality drops |
| Circuit | 5–8 stations | General fitness, fat loss, ≤30 min | Large | Weak for max strength |

### Weekly pattern balance (Practitioner defaults)

| Pattern | Weekly hard sets (general trainee) |
|---|---|
| Horizontal push : horizontal pull | 1 : 1 to 1 : 1.5 |
| Vertical push : vertical pull | 1 : 1 to 1 : 2 |
| Knee-dominant : hip-dominant | 1 : 1 (hinge slightly lower systemic fatigue cost via RDL/hip thrust choice) |
| Unilateral share of lower-body work | 20–40% |
| Core / anti-rotation | 2–3 exercises per week, 6–10 sets |
| Carries | 1–2 per week, 2–4 sets of 20–40 m or 30–60 s |

## Decision rules

**Warm-up**
- IF session starts with a heavy compound (≥85% 1RM or ≤5 reps) THEN do 5 min general + 3–4 ramp sets with load rising to ~85–90% of top set, fewer reps as load rises [S16][S17][S18].
- IF session is accessory-only/light THEN 3–5 min general + 1–2 light sets of first exercise.
- IF user wants stretching before training THEN use dynamic movement, or static holds ≤30–45 s followed by dynamic/specific work [S2][S3].
- IF explosive/power work is first THEN add 2–3 low-amplitude jumps/throws sets as potentiation; no static stretching before.
- IF time ≤30 min THEN keep warm-up to ~5 min but never skip ramp sets for the first lift.

**Ordering**
- IF goal = strength THEN order: power → main lift → secondary → accessories [S5][S14].
- IF goal = hypertrophy and a muscle is lagging THEN place its exercise first [S5] (hypertrophy is order-tolerant, so priority placement is a safe lever).
- IF two exercises are equally important THEN alternate which comes first week to week.
- IF an exercise is technical (Olympic lift, heavy squat) THEN do it before fatigue-inducing work.
- IF grip-limited pull (deadlift) THEN do it before heavy rows/pulls.
- IF core work is heavy-bracing dependent (e.g., hard ab wheel before squats) THEN place it after main lifts.
- IF conditioning is included THEN place after lifting, unless endurance is the primary goal of that day.

**Volume / exercises**
- IF a muscle already has ≥6–8 hard sets in this session THEN stop adding sets for it; move volume to another day [S6].
- IF weekly target for a muscle > ~10–12 sets THEN split it across ≥2 sessions [S6][S9].
- IF time available is 30 min THEN 3–5 exercises, antagonist supersets, 10–14 sets.
- IF time available is 45 min THEN 4–6 exercises, 1 main + supersetted accessories.
- IF time available is 60 min THEN 5–7 exercises, 18–22 sets.
- IF time available is 75–90 min THEN 6–9 exercises, 22–34 sets; do not exceed 10 sets/muscle; add rest to main lifts (3–5 min [S14]).
- IF user is a beginner THEN 3–5 exercises, 2–3 sets each, full body, 6–15RM [S8].
- IF user only has ~2 sessions/week of 30 min THEN include 1 squat/leg-press pattern, 1 push, 1 pull each session, ≥4 sets/muscle/week [S8].

**Pairing**
- IF time-constrained THEN antagonist or non-competing supersets for accessories first, compounds second [S7][S8].
- IF the lift is a heavy main lift (≤5 reps, 85%+) THEN do NOT superset; keep full rest.
- IF the user dislikes feeling gassed or form degrades THEN revert to straight sets.
- IF goal is maximal strength THEN limit supersets to accessories (strength benefits less than hypertrophy [S8]).
- IF the paired exercises share the same muscle THEN expect ~lower volume load; reduce load by 5–10% or use as finisher [S7].

**Rest**
- IF isolation/hypertrophy accessory THEN rest ≥60 s, preferably 90 s for compound accessories [S12].
- IF heavy 1–6RM THEN 3–5 min [S14].

**Balance**
- IF the plan has more than ~1.5× horizontal/vertical pressing volume vs pulling THEN add pulling sets (Practitioner [S20]).
- IF the plan has no hinge or no single-leg work in the week THEN add one hinge, one unilateral lower exercise.
- IF user has a side-to-side strength asymmetry or wants single-leg strength THEN add unilateral work [S19].
- IF user needs max bilateral strength THEN prioritise bilateral lifts [S19].

**Cool-down**
- IF the user wants it THEN 3–5 min easy walk/bike plus optional light stretching; mark as optional [S10][S11].
- IF user asks whether stretching reduces soreness THEN say evidence shows no clinically important effect [S10][S11].

## Common mistakes

- Static stretching 60+ s as the only warm-up before heavy or explosive work [S2].
- No ramp sets, jumping straight to a working weight; or excessive warm-up fatigue (too many near-max singles).
- Putting isolation/core before the main compound lift.
- Exceeding ~10 sets per muscle in one session ("destroying" a muscle once a week) and assuming it's necessary [S6].
- Stuffing 10+ exercises into 45 min with 30-s rests, collapsing load and reps (rest <60 s lowers volume load [S12]).
- Supersetting heavy compounds that share the same muscle (volume load drops [S7]).
- Push-heavy plans (bench/OHP/dips/flyes) with a few token pulling sets [S20].
- No hinge or no single-leg or no carries/core across the week.
- Counting only "main" sets and ignoring synergist work (e.g., bench also trains triceps) when estimating per-session volume [S6].
- Prescribing cool-down stretching as necessary for recovery [S10][S11].
- Time budgets that ignore rest and transition time.

## Controversies & open questions

- **Per-session volume cap**: the ~2 (strength) / ~11 (hypertrophy, fractional) figures come from a non-peer-reviewed meta-regression [S6]; high-volume session data are scarce. Many coaches cite 6–10 direct sets/muscle/session as practitioner heuristics (not verified in a source here).
- **Exercise order**: effect on hypertrophy appears nil in short studies; long-term effects unknown (<12 wk) [S5].
- **Static stretching in warm-up**: older meta-analyses say avoid; Behm 2016 argues it is fine in a full warm-up and may help with strain injury risk; dynamic stretching effects on injury are unstudied [S2][S3].
- **Warm-up sets**: number, load and rest not established; evidence mixed [S16][S17]. RAMP is untested as a whole [S4].
- **Rest length**: >60 s vs ≥3 min for hypertrophy remains debated; a viral "3 min = 93% more growth" claim isn't supported by the meta-analytic data [S12].
- **Superset long-term outcomes**: short studies; effects in advanced lifters unclear; MED guidance may not apply to athletes [S7][S8].
- **Push:pull ratio, knee:hip ratio, carries, anti-rotation**: no trial-tested ratios found; Practitioner.
- **Cool-down**: no evidence either way on psychological relaxation/ROM benefits; DOMS evidence is low-quality [S11].
- **2026 ACSM position stand**: may supersede some numbers cited from 2009 [S15]; I did not retrieve its specifics.

## Example session templates

Notation: sets × reps @ RIR, rest. SS = superset (alternate sets, rest after each pair).

### T1. 45-min full body (beginner/intermediate, 3×/wk)
| Block | Exercise category | Prescription |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up (6 min) | 3 min bike/row + 4 dynamic drills + 2 ramp sets of squat | — |
| A | Squat or leg press | 3 × 6–10 @2, 2–3 min |
| B1 / B2 (SS) | Horizontal press / horizontal row | 3 × 8–12 @1–2, 90 s after pair |
| C1 / C2 (SS) | Hip hinge (RDL) / vertical pull (pulldown) | 2–3 × 8–12 @2, 90 s |
| D1 / D2 (SS) | Lateral raise / core anti-extension | 2 × 12–15 / 2 × 30 s, 60 s |
| Cool-down | optional 3 min walk | — |

### T2. 30-min minimal-dose full body (2×/wk)
| Block | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Warm-up 4 min | 2 min easy cardio + 1–2 ramp sets of first lift |
| SS1: Squat/leg press + row | 3 × 8–12 @2, 90 s |
| SS2: Press (push-up/bench) + pulldown/pull-up | 3 × 8–12 @1–2, 90 s |
| Finisher: RDL or hip thrust | 2 × 10–12 @2, 90 s |
Per [S8]: ≥4 weekly sets/muscle with 6–15RM.

### T3. 60-min upper (hypertrophy, upper/lower 4×/wk)
| Block | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Warm-up 8 min | Raise 3 min, band/arm circles, 3 ramp sets bench |
| A. Horizontal press (main) | 4 × 5–8 @1–2, 3 min |
| B. Horizontal row | 4 × 6–10 @1–2, 2 min |
| C1/C2 SS: Incline DB press / chest-supported vertical pull | 3 × 8–12 @1, 90 s |
| D1/D2 SS: Lateral raise / rear-delt fly | 3 × 12–20 @0–1, 60–75 s |
| E1/E2 SS: Biceps curl / triceps extension | 3 × 10–15 @0–1, 60 s |
| Core | Pallof press 2 × 10/side |

### T4. 75-min lower (strength + hypertrophy)
| Block | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Warm-up 10 min | Raise 4 min, hip/ankle mobility, 4 ramp sets squat |
| Power (optional) | Box jumps/med-ball throws 3 × 3, 1–2 min |
| A. Back squat (main) | 4 × 4–6 @1–2, 3–4 min |
| B. RDL / hinge | 3 × 6–10 @2, 2–3 min |
| C. Split squat / lunge (unilateral) | 3 × 8–12 per leg @1–2, 90 s |
| D1/D2 SS: Leg curl / calf raise | 3 × 10–15, 60–90 s |
| E. Core: loaded carry + ab wheel | carry 3 × 30–40 m; wheel 2 × 8–12 |
| Conditioning (optional) | 5–10 min easy bike |

### T5. 90-min strength day (advanced, 3–4 d/wk)
| Block | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Warm-up 12 min | Full RAMP, 5 ramp sets to top set [S4] |
| A. Main lift (e.g., squat) | 5 × 3–5 @1–2, 4–5 min |
| B. Secondary (pause squat / front squat) | 3 × 5–8 @2, 3 min |
| C. Press/pull complement (antagonist SS) | 4 × 6–10, 2 min |
| D. Accessories (3 exercises, SS) | 3 × 8–15, 90 s |
| E. Core + carries | 3 sets |
