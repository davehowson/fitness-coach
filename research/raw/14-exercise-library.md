# Exercise Library for Workout Generation

> Research agent output · Topic 14 · 2026-10-08

## Executive summary

- **Library = filterable catalog.** Filter by pattern → equipment → skill → fatigue/axial cost → role. Every pattern below has a populated table (Pattern tables P1–P11, isolation tables I1–I12, power, conditioning, warm-up).
- **Ratings in the tables (skill, fatigue, axial load, role, rep range) are coaching-consensus defaults** `Practitioner`, built on biomechanics and the stimulus-to-fatigue-ratio (SFR) framework [S14]. No trial rates exercises on "systemic fatigue". Treat as defaults, not measured values.
- **Muscle-length matters for isolation choice.** Overhead triceps extension beat pushdown for triceps growth (12 wk, MRI, n=21; long head +28.5% vs +19.6%) [S1] `Moderate`. Seated leg curl beat lying for biarticular hamstrings (~14% vs ~9% volume gain) [S2] `Moderate`. Standing calf raise beat seated for gastrocnemius [S9] `Moderate`. Lengthened-bias is the tie-breaker when two options are otherwise equal. Contradicting evidence: a lengthened-partial advantage is not established (CI crosses 0) [S5][S6] `Contested`.
- **Glutes: hip thrust ≈ back squat for gluteal hypertrophy** (MRI, 9 wk, untrained), but squat grew more quads and adductors [S3] `Moderate`. Hip thrust has higher glute EMG, but EMG did not predict growth [S3][S13]. Use both: squat/hinge as main lift, hip thrust as glute accessory/main.
- **Quads need more than squats:** leg extension grew all three rectus femoris sites; Smith squat grew only central vastus lateralis (equal volume) [S4] `Moderate`. IF quad hypertrophy is a goal THEN include one knee-extension exercise.
- **Regional differences exist for curls** (incline favored proximal, preacher distal; differences ≈0.1 cm) [S8] `Moderate`/small. Variety of 2–3 exercises per muscle is reasonable; systematic (not random) variation is better than frequent rotation [S7] `Moderate`.
- **Main lifts** = big multi-joint patterns (squat, hinge, press, row/pull variants); **accessories** = machines, cables, isolation. Lower fatigue-cost options (leg press, chest-supported row, machines) let you add volume without wrecking recovery [S14] `Practitioner`.
- **Warm-up** follows RAMP (Raise, Activate/Mobilise, Potentiate) [S10]. Plyometric/power work goes early in session, low contacts, high quality [S12].
- **No medical advice.** Safety notes are general; refer pain/injury to a clinician.

## Core concepts

**Generic → specific.**

1. **Movement patterns** group exercises that train the same joint actions: squat, hinge, lunge/single-leg, horizontal/vertical push, horizontal/vertical pull, carry, core (anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral-flexion, flexion). Weekly balance across patterns keeps plans complete (see Topic 04 weekly pattern balance).
2. **Primary vs secondary muscle.** Primary = prime mover, counts as 1 set; secondary = synergist, counts as 0.5 set under fractional counting (Topic 02). The "Secondary" column is for that counting. Lists are typical, not exhaustive.
3. **Skill level:** Beginner = learnable in 1–3 sessions with low injury risk; Intermediate = needs coaching cues/practice weeks; Advanced = technical (Olympic derivatives, pull-up variants with load, deep unilateral under load).
4. **Systemic fatigue cost:** Low = local fatigue only (isolation, machines); Medium = multi-joint, moderate load or supported; High = heavy axial/whole-body (conventional deadlift, back squat near failure, Olympic lifts). Concept from SFR (Israetel/RP) [S14].
5. **Axial/spinal load:** compressive load through the spine from bar/weight on shoulders or hands hanging (Low = supported/seated/prone; High = bar on back or heavy deadlift).
6. **Role:** *Main* (heaviest, first, 3–5 sets), *Secondary* (second compound, 6–12 reps), *Accessory* (single-joint or machine, 8–20 reps) — matches Topic 04's block definitions.
7. **Stimulus-to-fatigue notes:** machines/cables/supported variants (leg press, chest-supported row) give high local stimulus with low spinal/balance demand; the trade-off is less transfer and less "free" stabilizer work [S14] `Practitioner`.
8. **Stretch-mediated bias:** exercises loading the muscle at long length (overhead triceps, incline curl, seated leg curl, RDL, deep squat, standing calf raise) tend to give equal or more growth in head-to-head trials [S1][S2][S9][S6]. Evidence is from single small trials, mostly untrained participants.

## Key findings

1. **Overhead triceps extension > pushdown for triceps hypertrophy.** 12 wk, 5×10, 2×/wk, MRI, 21 untrained adults, within-person: whole triceps +19.9% (overhead) vs +13.9% (arm at side); long head +28.5% vs +19.6%; strength gains similar [S1]. `Moderate` (one trial, untrained, small n). Popular reports of "40% more" use different statistics; use the whole-muscle figures.
2. **Seated leg curl > lying leg curl for hamstring growth (biarticular heads).** 12 wk, 20 participants, one leg each: greater growth in semitendinosus, semimembranosus, biceps femoris long head; no difference for short head; lying favored sartorius; strength gains slightly higher for seated [S2]. `Moderate`. Secondary summaries are used here (original paper not fetched); percentages (14% vs 9%) as reported by BarBend.
3. **Standing > seated calf raise for gastrocnemius.** 12 wk, n=14, within-person MRI: lateral gastrocnemius +12.4% vs +1.7%, medial +9.2% vs +0.6%; soleus similar (2.1% vs 2.9%) [S9]. `Moderate`. Implication: standing (or straight-knee) calf work is the default; seated is optional for soleus.
4. **Hip thrust vs squat for glutes: similar glute hypertrophy.** 9 wk, n=34 untrained, MRI: similar glute growth; squat grew quads (+3.6 cm²) and adductors (+2.5 cm²) more; exercise-specific strength gains; deadlift strength transferred equally [S3]. `Moderate`. Hip thrust gives higher glute EMG (upper GMax mean 69.5% vs 29.4% MVIC) [S13], but EMG did not consistently predict hypertrophy [S3]. `Moderate` (EMG ≠ growth).
5. **Quadriceps need regional coverage.** Equal-volume trial: leg extension grew rectus femoris at proximal, central, distal sites; Smith squat grew only central vastus lateralis [S4]. A full-depth vs half-depth squat study found no between-group quad difference but favored hip extensor growth with depth (as summarized in [S6] search results). `Moderate`/`Contested`. Rule: squat/leg press for vasti + leg extension (or similar) for rectus femoris.
6. **Incline vs preacher curl: regional difference.** 8 wk, 63 young women: incline → greater proximal elbow flexor thickness (+0.08 cm), preacher → greater distal (+0.10 cm); strength gains exercise-specific [S8]. `Moderate`, effect small. Use both across a mesocycle if arm size is a priority.
7. **Range of motion:** full ROM vs partial ROM → trivial advantage for full ROM (SMD 0.12); subgroup of partial at long muscle length trended favorable (−0.28, CI −0.81–0.16, not significant) [S5]. Systematic review: lengthened training (partial or full) tends to be best for quadriceps, biceps, triceps [S6]. `Moderate`/`Contested`. Default full ROM; lengthened partials as an optional intensifier on isolation.
8. **Exercise variation:** some systematic variation may help regional adaptation; excessive random variation or frequent rotation may blunt gains (8 studies, n=241 young men) [S7]. `Moderate` (thin). Rule: keep core exercises stable 4–8 weeks; rotate accessories at block ends.
9. **Multiple exercises per muscle** are justified by regional/head differences (findings 1,2,4,5,6) — each is a single small trial, so `Moderate` at best; weekly sets per muscle still drive volume (Topic 02).
10. **Core exercises:** curl-up, side bridge, bird dog (McGill "big 3") produce relatively low spine loading compared with sit-ups [S11] `Practitioner`/`Moderate`. Anti-rotation (Pallof) EMG shows movement-type-dependent oblique/erector activity, small exploratory study [S16] `Practitioner`.
11. **Plyometric dose:** meta-analysis of vertical-jump programs: >10 weeks, >20 sessions, >50 jumps/session maximized the probability of significant gains [S12]; a 2025 meta-analysis reported <900 total contacts was enough to improve jump height (as summarized in search results; original not fetched). `Moderate`/`Contested`. Keep early-session, fresh, ≤ 40–80 contacts per session as a practitioner default.
12. **RAMP warm-up** (Raise → Activate & Mobilise → Potentiate) is a UKSCA/NSCA-endorsed framework, not an RCT-proven superior method [S10]. `Practitioner`.

## Decision rules

1. IF building a session THEN pick pattern first, then within pattern pick by (a) equipment available, (b) skill level ≤ trainee level, (c) fatigue/axial fit to session position, (d) injury constraints.
2. IF the trainee is beginner THEN restrict to Skill = B, prefer machines and goblet/DB variants; avoid conventional deadlift, front squat, overhead press as main until technique is established.
3. IF training time is short (≤ 45 min) THEN choose main lifts with high muscle coverage (leg press/hack/squat, bench/DB press, row/pulldown, RDL) and superset non-competing pairs (e.g. quad + horizontal pull, biceps + triceps, calves + core) (Topic 04).
4. IF fatigue budget is tight (e.g. multiple hard sessions per week, deficit, poor recovery) THEN prefer Low/Medium fatigue + Low axial options (leg press for squat, chest-supported row, machine press, hip thrust for hinge) [S14] `Practitioner`.
5. IF quad hypertrophy is a goal THEN include one squat/leg-press pattern AND one leg-extension-type exercise [S4].
6. IF hamstring hypertrophy is a goal THEN include one hip-hinge (RDL) AND one knee-flexion exercise; prefer seated leg curl [S2].
7. IF glute hypertrophy is a goal THEN pair squat/lunge/hinge with hip thrust (or bridge); both ≈ equal for glute growth [S3].
8. IF triceps hypertrophy is a goal THEN lead with overhead extension (cable or DB), then add pushdown/dip as volume needs [S1].
9. IF biceps hypertrophy is a goal THEN use incline curl + (preacher or hammer) curl; vary across blocks [S8].
10. IF calves are a goal THEN use standing calf raise (full stretch) first, seated optional [S9].
11. IF trainee has no equipment THEN: push-up family; pike push-up; inverted row/towel row; split squat/step-up/Bulgarian on furniture; single-leg RDL with bag; glute bridge; slider hamstring curl; plank family; band options if available.
12. IF trainee has dumbbells only THEN: DB bench/floor press, DB OHP, 1-arm row, goblet/Bulgarian/DB RDL, DB hip thrust, lateral raise, curl, overhead extension, farmer carries.
13. IF a movement causes joint pain (e.g. shoulder with overhead press, knee with deep lunge) THEN swap to a pattern-equivalent with better joint angle (landmine press, reverse lunge, box squat, leg press) and recommend seeing a clinician for persistent pain; do not diagnose.
14. IF plyometrics/power are in the session THEN place them after warm-up, before heavy lifts, keep contacts 20–80 per session and rest full (1–3 min); skip when sore or very fatigued [S12].
15. IF Olympic lifts requested by non-experienced trainee THEN substitute KB swing, jump squat or med-ball throw; Olympic lifts need coaching [S15].
16. IF impact is a concern (joint issues, heavy bodyweight, deconditioned) THEN choose Low-impact conditioning (bike, rower, sled, ski erg, carries) over burpees/running/jump rope.
17. IF the plan has ≥ 4 weeks THEN keep main lifts constant and rotate accessories at block end; don't swap every session [S7].
18. IF supersetting THEN pair antagonist (biceps/triceps, push/pull) or non-competing (upper/lower) exercises; avoid pairing two heavy axial lifts or two grip-heavy pulls.
19. IF core is scheduled THEN include ≥ 1 anti-extension and ≥ 1 anti-rotation/lateral exercise per week, flexion optional; carries count as anti-lateral-flexion.
20. IF fractional counting is used THEN give 1 set to primary, 0.5 to listed secondary (see Topic 02), and don't count a muscle's secondary role if its involvement is minor (e.g. biceps in rows is ~0.5, chest in overhead press is minor).

### Main lift vs accessory vs superset quick list

- **Main-lift candidates:** back/front/safety-bar squat, hack squat, leg press (hypertrophy), deadlift (conventional/trap/RDL), hip thrust, barbell/DB bench, incline press, OHP/seated DB press, barbell/chest-supported row, pull-up/chin-up/pulldown.
- **Secondary:** split squat/Bulgarian, lunges, RDL (as secondary), DB press variants, cable row, close-grip bench, dips.
- **Accessory:** all isolation tables above + core + carries.
- **Good superset pairs:** biceps ↔ triceps; chest press ↔ row; lateral raise ↔ rear-delt fly (non-competing); leg extension ↔ leg curl; calf raise ↔ core; Pallof ↔ push-up; hip thrust ↔ pull-up.
- **Poor superset pairs:** squat ↔ deadlift; heavy row ↔ heavy RDL (back fatigue); two grip-heavy pulls.

## Common mistakes

- Treating tables' fatigue/skill ratings as measured facts — they are consensus defaults.
- Selecting by "muscle activation" EMG alone: EMG did not predict hip thrust vs squat hypertrophy [S3].
- Only squats for quads; ignoring rectus femoris [S4].
- Only pushdowns for triceps; ignoring long head [S1].
- Seated calf raise as only calf exercise [S9].
- Rotating all exercises weekly; no progressive overload on any lift [S7].
- High axial loading on consecutive days (squat + deadlift + OHP + row heavy).
- Skipping carries and anti-rotation core entirely.
- Prescribing Olympic lifts/depth jumps to beginners without coach.
- Using burpees/sprints as finisher for people with joint concerns.
- Stacking too many secondary muscles into volume counts (double counting).

## Controversies & open questions

- **Fatigue/axial ratings** — no direct measure; "SFR" is a practitioner heuristic and is criticized as not evidenced [S14].
- **Lengthened-position training** — small trials, mostly untrained; partial-ROM advantage CI crosses zero [S5][S6]. `Contested`.
- **Hip thrust vs squat for glutes** — equal growth in untrained 9-wk MRI [S3]; trained populations/longer periods unstudied.
- **Seated vs lying leg curl and incline vs preacher** — single trials of 8–12 weeks; effect on long-term hypertrophy unknown. Percentages vary between secondary reports.
- **Rectus femoris leg extension** — RF growth with extension vs squat is based on one trial [S4]; ideal hip angle unknown.
- **Plyometric dose** — contact counts differ between meta-analyses (50+/session vs <900 total) [S12].
- **Core training direct benefits** — evidence limited; anti-rotation EMG small [S16].
- **Unfetched originals:** Several studies were summarized through secondary pages only (see Research log); verify before quoting precise numbers.
