# Cardio & Conditioning

No medical treatment advice here; people with cardiac/metabolic disease or symptoms should get clinician clearance first.

## Executive summary
- Default adult dose: 150–300 min/wk moderate OR 75–150 min/wk vigorous OR an equivalent mix (1 vigorous min ≈ 2 moderate min) [S1][S2]. Strong.
- For general fitness, most weekly minutes should be easy (can talk, ≤ first ventilatory threshold, ~RPE 3–4/10) plus 1–2 hard sessions. ~80/20 is what elite endurance athletes do [S3]; evidence it is *optimal* is moderate and mostly in trained athletes [S4].
- Polarized vs. other distributions: small advantage for polarized on VO2peak (SMD 0.24) in meta-analysis, mostly trained athletes [S4]. Recreational people: any sensible distribution works; adherence dominates. Moderate.
- HIIT/SIT improve VO2max about as much as or more than MICT in much less time; fat-loss differences between HIIT and MICT are small [S5][S6][S7][S8]. Time-efficiency is the real HIIT advantage.
- "Zone 2" is useful for volume with low fatigue, but evidence does not show it is uniquely superior for mitochondria/VO2max [S9]. Contested.
- Talk test tracks first ventilatory threshold reasonably (validated mainly in cardiac pts) [S10]. Use RPE + talk test as primary field tools; %HRmax formulas are imprecise.
- Concurrent training: interference on max strength and hypertrophy is ~nil in the 2022 meta-analysis (SMD −0.06 and ~0); explosive strength is mildly reduced (SMD −0.28), mainly with same-session, <3 h gap [S11][S12]. Lifters can do 2–3 cardio sessions/wk safely.
- The "10% rule" has no good support: an RCT of a 10%-graded plan vs standard plan found equal injury rates (~20%) [S13]. Use it as a soft ceiling, not a law.
- Programming trick for lifters: bike or incline-walk/row for low-impact cardio, put hard intervals away from heavy leg day, keep Z2 sessions short-to-moderate.

## Core concepts
**Energy systems (Practitioner/textbook; no single source retrieved).**
| System | Fuel | Duration of dominance | Typical training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phosphagen (ATP-PCr) | creatine phosphate | 0–~10 s | max sprints, 5–10 s reps, long rest |
| Glycolytic (anaerobic) | glucose/glycogen | ~10 s–2 min | 30 s–2 min hard reps, SIT, metcons |
| Oxidative (aerobic) | carbs + fat | >2 min, dominant by ~3 min | steady state, tempo, VO2max intervals |
All three operate simultaneously; training targets which is the limiter. Aerobic training → higher stroke volume, capillary density, mitochondrial content, fat oxidation, lower HR at a given speed. Anaerobic/sprint work → buffering capacity, glycolytic enzymes, power, and (with SIT) surprisingly large VO2max gains in novices [S7].

**Intensity markers.** First lactate/ventilatory threshold (LT1/VT1, ~2 mmol/L) = upper edge of "easy"; second threshold (LT2/VT2, MLSS/critical power region) = upper edge of "sustainable hard"; VO2max = ceiling. Seiler 3-zone model: Z1 < VT1, Z2 between VT1 and VT2, Z3 > VT2 [S3]. Seiler 5-zone: Z2 ≈ 72–82% HRmax, 1.5–2.5 mmol/L [S9 summary of S3]. Note the confusing naming: popular "zone 2" = Seiler 3-zone **Zone 1** (below VT1, <~2 mmol/L) [S9].

**Zone 2 definition used here:** highest steady effort that is still fully conversational, near/below VT1, lactate ≈ 1.5–2 mmol/L, ~RPE 3–4/10, roughly 60–75% HRmax (varies widely between people).

**Modalities.** Running (highest impact, highest specificity for runners), cycling (low impact, lowest interference with leg strength per Wilson-era data, see S12 caveat), rowing (full body, high spinal/back load at intensity), swimming (non-weight-bearing, technique-limited), rucking (weighted walking: pack adds ~20% O2 cost and ~10% HR at 20 kg [S14, secondary]), sled/assault bike (concentric, low soreness—Practitioner).

## Key findings
1. **Public-health dose.** Adults: 150–300 min/wk moderate, or 75–150 vigorous, plus muscle-strengthening ≥2 d/wk; some activity beats none; more gives added benefit [S1]. ACSM 2011: ≥150 min/wk moderate, vigorous RPE 14–17 (6–20 scale); weight loss/maintenance generally needs more [S2 (partly secondary)]. **Strong.**
2. **Training intensity distribution in elite endurance athletes** is ~80% of sessions low intensity (<2 mM) and ~20% high intensity; this is descriptive, and Seiler notes intensification studies in trained athletes show no convincing benefit of more HIT [S3]. **Practitioner/Moderate.**
3. **Polarized vs. others.** 2024 meta-analysis (17 studies, n=437): polarized favored for VO2peak, SMD 0.24 (95% CI 0.01–0.48), I²=0% [S4]. Earlier meta-analysis (Rosenblat 2019) compared polarized vs threshold in endurance athletes [S4b, details not retrieved]. Effect is small; trials short (typically 4–12 wk) and in trained athletes. **Moderate.** For recreational people no direct trials; treat as extrapolation.
4. **HIIT vs MICT, VO2max.** Meta-analyses in patients show HIIT +1.5–1.8 mL/kg/min over MICT (CAD n=472; HF n=557, very low quality evidence) (secondary) [S5]. Helgerud 2007: 4×4 min at 85–95% HRmax, 3 min active recovery, 3 d/wk × 8 wk raised VO2max ~7% in moderately trained men vs ~0 for volume-matched moderate work (exact % varies by secondary source) [S6 (secondary)]. **Moderate.**
5. **SIT.** Wingate-style protocol (4–6 × 30 s all-out, ~4 min recovery): VO2max effect +0.69 SMD vs no-exercise controls but only 0.04 vs endurance-training controls (16 RCTs) [S7]. Time-efficient but unpleasant; not better than endurance training. **Moderate.**
6. **Fat loss.** In overweight/obese adults 13 studies: HIIT and MICT both reduce fat mass with no significant difference; HIIT ~40% less time [S8]. Later reviews agree (some show small HIIT edge for waist or %fat) [S8b, secondary]. Fat loss is mostly set by energy balance; cardio is a tool to raise expenditure. **Moderate.**
7. **Zone 2.** Narrative review (Storoschuk, Gibala, Gurd 2025): no substantive evidence Z2 is superior to higher intensities for mitochondrial/fat-oxidation adaptation; sedentary people benefit; at equal volume, gains in VO2max are greater above Z2; Z2 alone generally insufficient to raise VO2max in active/trained people [S9]. **Contested** (San-Millán's lactate-based claims are practitioner/clinical-opinion, not retrieved from primary sources).
8. **Talk test.** Systematic review (10 studies, 414 pts, mostly CAD): last "comfortable talk" stage corresponds to VT1 (HR/VO2 not different); reliability OK but only one study analysed it properly; protocol not standardized [S10]. **Moderate** for validity as a VT1 proxy.
9. **Concurrent training.** Schumann 2022 (43 studies): max strength SMD −0.06 (CI −0.20–0.09, n.s.), hypertrophy ≈ no effect (reported ~−0.01, secondary), explosive strength SMD −0.28 (CI −0.48 to −0.08); larger when aerobic and strength are in the same session / <3 h apart (secondary) [S11][S12]. Wilson 2012 (21 studies) earlier reported larger interference—greater with higher endurance frequency, longer sessions, and running vs cycling (secondary) [S12]. Sex/training-status moderators reported smaller interference in females (secondary, low confidence) [S12]. **Strong→Moderate** that interference is small for strength/size at sensible doses; **Contested** for power.
10. **10% rule.** GRONORUN RCT (n=532 novice runners): 13-wk graded plan vs 8-wk standard plan, injury 20.8% vs 20.3% — no benefit [S13]. Cohort data: >30% weekly volume jumps associated with more injury than ~22% (secondary) [S13b]. Large wearable data suggests week-to-week % change barely predicts injury (secondary blog; unverified). **Moderate** that a strict 10% cap is unnecessary; **Practitioner** that large single-week jumps (>30%) and big single-session spikes are risky.
11. **Load carriage.** Pack of ~22% of body mass lifts HR from ~71% to ~83% HRmax in soldiers; O2 cost plateaus above ~44% BM while HR keeps climbing (secondary) [S14]. Speed matters more than load for energy cost. **Moderate (secondary).**

## Numeric guidelines

### Intensity zones (use 3 markers; pick whichever you can measure)
| Zone (Seiler 3 / common label) | %HRmax | %HRR | RPE (0–10) | Talk test | Lactate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 easy / "Zone 2" popular | ~60–75 | ~40–60 | 3–4 | full sentences | <2 mM | ≤VT1 [S3][S9][S10] |
| Z2 tempo/threshold | ~76–88 | ~60–80 | 5–7 | short phrases | 2–4 mM | between VT1–VT2 |
| Z3 VO2max / hard | ~88–95+ | 80–95 | 8–9 | single words | >4 mM | >VT2 |
| Sprint | n/a (HR lags) | | 10 | none | | all-out ≤30 s |
%HR bands are Practitioner defaults (HRmax = 208 − 0.7×age or measured); individual VT1 can sit anywhere ~55–80% HRmax. Prefer talk test/RPE to calibrate [S10].

## Example weekly cardio plans (add-on to resistance training; E = easy Z1, H = hard interval)
**2 sessions/wk (≈ 60–90 min):** Day A: 30–45 min E. Day B: 4×4 min H (or 8×30/30) + warm-up/cool-down (~35 min). Place away from heavy leg day (≥24 h, ideally >6 h from lifting).
**3 sessions/wk (≈ 100–150 min):** Mon E 40; Wed H (4×4) ; Sat E 45–60 (long). Optional 10–15 min easy bike post-lifting.
**4 sessions/wk (≈ 150–210 min):** E 30–40; H intervals 30–40; tempo or E 30–45; long E 60. ≈ 80/20.
**5 sessions/wk (≈ 200–300 min):** E 30–45 ×3 (one is long 60–75); H VO2max intervals; threshold/tempo or fartlek. Only if lifting volume is moderate; cut lifting volume by ~10–20% if recovery lags.
For fat loss: same templates plus daily step goal; HIIT optional. For rucking, replace one E session with a 45–60 min ruck at ≤10–15% BM.

## Decision rules
- IF beginner/sedentary THEN start 3 × 20–30 min Z1 (talk test passes), progress to 150 min/wk over 6–8 weeks, add intervals later. [S1][S9]
- IF goal = general health THEN target 150–300 moderate-equivalent min/wk and 2 d strength; count 1 vigorous min = 2 moderate. [S1]
- IF time-poor (<3 h/wk) THEN use 1–2 HIIT/SIT sessions (4×4 or 8×30/30) + 1 easy session; same VO2max/fat-loss yield per time. [S5][S7][S8]
- IF user has no HR monitor THEN use talk test: full sentences = Z1, short phrases = tempo, single words = interval. [S10]
- IF user says "I must do Zone 2" THEN explain Z2 is a good base and low-fatigue volume builder, not a unique magic zone; keep ≥1 harder session if VO2max matters. [S9]
- IF goal = endurance performance (recreational) THEN ~80% easy / ~20% hard, 1–2 hard sessions; IF trained and plateaued THEN polarized slight edge; pyramidal acceptable. [S3][S4]
- IF goal = fat loss THEN pick the cardio they will keep doing; prioritize energy deficit and steps; HIIT ≈ MICT for fat mass. [S8]
- IF goal = strength/hypertrophy THEN cardio 2–3 sessions, 20–45 min, mostly low-impact (bike/incline walk/row), ≤1 interval session/wk; expect negligible effect on size/strength. [S11]
- IF goal includes explosive power/sprinting THEN keep cardio/intervals ≥6 h from power or heavy leg sessions (or separate days); minimize same-session endurance. [S11][S12]
- IF same-day lifting and cardio THEN lift first if strength/hypertrophy is priority; if cardio quality is priority do it first or separate ≥3–6 h. Low-intensity cardio after lifting is fine. [S11] Practitioner for order.
- IF legs are sore/heavy lift day THEN swap run for bike/swim/upper-body-light cardio or rest.
- IF running volume increase planned THEN cap at ~10% as soft target, never >30% in one week; IF novice prone to shin/knee pain THEN substitute cycling for part of volume. [S13]
- IF mileage plateau/fatigue/HR high at same pace/sleep poor THEN take deload week (−20–30%) and drop hard session. Practitioner.
- IF ruck THEN start 10% BM (≤ 10 lb/5 kg), 30 min; add ~5% BM or 10 min every 1–2 weeks; speed raises cost more than load. [S14]
- IF cardio session > 60 min or HIIT with weights back-to-back THEN include fueling (carbs) and warm-up; reduce next-day leg volume. Practitioner.
- IF user reports chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness THEN stop and refer to clinician (no coaching around this).

## Common mistakes
- Making every cardio session "moderately hard" (grey-zone) — too hard to recover from, too easy to drive VO2max.
- Prescribing HIIT 4–6 days/wk or on top of heavy leg days.
- Prescribing %HRmax with the 220−age formula as exact; ignoring talk test/RPE.
- Treating "Zone 2" as magic or ignoring its inconsistent definitions.
- Enforcing a rigid 10% rule (or ignoring large jumps).
- Long running for hypertrophy-focused lifters with leg-heavy training; same-session sprint-intervals before squats.
- Using HIIT as the fat-loss answer without dealing with energy balance.
- No warm-up/cool-down, no progression or deload defined.
- Rucking too heavy too soon, or treating calories-burned calculator values as precise.

## Controversies & open questions
- Zone 2: unique mitochondrial/fat-oxidation benefits vs. just a high-volume low-stress base [S9].
- Polarized vs. pyramidal: small pooled edge for polarized, but short trials, trained athletes, heterogeneous definitions; may reflect session-RPE and duration confounding [S4].
- Interference: Wilson 2012 vs Schumann 2022; power vs hypertrophy; sex and training status moderators uncertain [S11][S12].
- 10% rule: benefits unproven; acute:chronic workload ratios show weak injury prediction (secondary).
- Whether HIIT truly outperforms MICT for fat loss (current: no) and for older/untrained people (VO2max gains vary; secondary).
- SIT sustainability and enjoyment; unsupervised adherence.
- No high-quality trial data for rucking vs. other modalities for long-term outcomes.
