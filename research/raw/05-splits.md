# Training Splits Catalog

> Research agent output · Topic 05 · 2026-10-08

## Executive summary
- A "split" is only a way to distribute weekly sets per muscle across days. **Weekly sets per muscle and effort matter more than the split label.** When weekly volume is equated, higher vs lower per-muscle frequency gives no significant hypertrophy difference (25-study meta-analysis) [S2] and no significant strength difference in volume-equated subgroups [S3][S4][S5]. Grade: Strong for hypertrophy, Moderate for strength.
- Frequency still matters indirectly: it lets you reach high weekly volume without junk volume in one session, and gives more practice on skills/lifts. 1x/week/muscle works fine at lower volume (<~10 sets/wk) [S5]; 2x/week/muscle is the practical default for most [S1][S2][S7].
- Direct split-vs-full-body RCTs (mostly 8–12 wk, small, often untrained) show similar results when volume is matched [S6][S9][S10]. No trial supports long-term superiority of any named split. Grade: Moderate (short duration).
- Pick the split by (a) days/week the person will actually keep, (b) desired per-muscle frequency (target ≥2x), (c) session length, (d) recovery tolerance, (e) preference. Adherence is the tiebreaker.
- Default mapping: 2–3 days → full body; 4 days → upper/lower; 5 days → upper/lower + PPL hybrid (ULPPL) or PHAT-style; 6 days → PPL x2 (or Arnold for high-tolerance); body-part split (1x/muscle) only for preference or high-volume specialization with ≥1 extra weekly hit on lagging muscles.
- ACSM 2009: novice 2–3 d/wk, intermediate 3–4, advanced 4–5 [S11]. Powerlifters average ~4 sessions/wk, bench most often, deadlift least [S14].
- Missed days: favor **rolling** (sequence-based) schedules for irregular lives; never double up to "catch up"; 1–2 missed sessions cause negligible loss [S15][S16].
- Most named-split details (PHUL, PHAT, Arnold, torso/limbs, AP) come from practitioner/app sources, not research. Labeled Practitioner.

## Core concepts
- **Split**: assignment of muscles/movement patterns to training days across a microcycle.
- **Per-muscle frequency**: sessions per week that train a muscle with meaningful direct or indirect sets. Count indirect work cautiously (e.g. triceps get ~½ credit from pressing; Practitioner).
- **Weekly volume**: hard sets per muscle per week. Dose-response is positive with diminishing returns for both hypertrophy and strength [S7].
- **Per-session volume ceiling** (Practitioner): ~6–10 hard sets/muscle/session before marginal returns drop; more when spread across sessions. A recent preprint explores per-session volume dose-response [S7].
- **Fixed-calendar split**: days tied to weekdays (Mon=Upper). **Rolling split**: workouts run in sequence regardless of weekday; rest days inserted by pattern or by need.
- **Overlap fatigue**: a day that trains muscles used as synergists on the next day (e.g. push day then shoulders day) reduces quality; rest-day and ordering rules aim to avoid it.
- **Frequency–volume coupling**: in non-volume-equated studies higher frequency wins mostly because it carries more volume [S1][S2][S3].

## Key findings
1. **Volume-equated frequency has no significant effect on hypertrophy.** 25 studies; null across resistance-trained subgroup, upper vs lower body, direct measures; non-equated studies favored higher frequency modestly. Grade: **Strong** [S2]. Earlier 2016 meta-analysis (10 studies, mostly non-volume-equated, many untrained) found ES 0.49 (≥2x) vs 0.30 (1x) [S1]; superseded in interpretation by [S2].
2. **Strength: frequency effect vanishes when volume equated.** Higher frequency correlated with strength gains overall; volume-equated subgroup p=0.421 [S3][S4]. A newer meta-analysis in trained lifters trended to "no real difference" [S5]. Grade: **Moderate–Strong**.
3. **Volume dose-response is positive and gradual-diminishing**; frequency effect for hypertrophy not consistently identifiable once volume is modeled, but frequency shows more identifiable effects for strength [S7] (preprint; secondary summary of numbers unverified). Grade: **Moderate**.
4. **Split vs full body RCTs**: 50 untrained women, 2x full-body vs 4x split, 12 wk — no between-group differences [S6]. 67 untrained men, 8 wk — similar adaptations at equal weekly sets [S10 via search summary; verify design]. Trained men: 3 vs 6 sessions/wk at equal volume gave similar hypertrophy/strength [S12]; other small trained-men studies reported slight edges for full-body (forearm flexor, biceps/quad thickness) with no measure favoring split routines [S8][S9]. Grade: **Moderate**, short trials.
5. **High-frequency (5 days/week/muscle) vs bro split (1x)**, volume-equated, well-trained men (2024 RCT per Henselmans review): full body matched or beat bro split in absolute terms; bro split had more soreness [S8]. Grade: **Moderate** (single trial; summary secondary).
6. **Powerlifters**: Norwegian 15-wk study—6 sessions/wk gained more squat/bench than 3 (≈11% vs 5–6%); deadlift no difference [S13]. Authors asked figures removed, so treat sizes with caution. Survey (n=548): ~4.25 sessions/wk; squat ~1.6x, bench ~2.5x, deadlift ~1.4x per week [S14]. Grade: **Moderate** (study) / **Practitioner** (survey).
7. **Fewer than ~10 sets/muscle/week → once-weekly is fine**; split volume across days when sets per muscle exceed what one session can do with quality [S5]. Grade: **Practitioner/Moderate**.
8. **Short breaks cost little.** 1–3 weeks off: minimal strength loss; meaningful decay appears at ~5–16 wk; strength-trained athletes lost ~7–12% over 8–12 wk inactivity; regaining is faster than building [S15][S16]. Grade: **Moderate**.
9. **ACSM 2009**: novice 2–3, intermediate 3–4, advanced 4–5 d/wk; split routines suggested for advanced lifters; 48-h recovery between sessions for the same muscle group suggested [S11]. Grade: **Strong (position stand)**, dated.
10. **Named splits (PPL, Arnold, PHUL, PHAT, torso/limbs, AP) have no head-to-head trials.** Their rationales are scheduling and fatigue-overlap management [S17][S18][S19][S20]. Grade: **Practitioner**.

## Decision rules
1. IF days/week ≤3 THEN use full body (A/B alternation) with ≥1 rest day between sessions. 
2. IF days/week = 4 AND goal is strength or general hypertrophy THEN upper/lower. IF arms/delts/calves are priority THEN torso/limbs. IF the person wants pattern-based balance and tolerates ordering complexity THEN anterior/posterior.
3. IF days/week = 5 AND intermediate THEN ULPPL or upper/lower + extra FB day; IF advanced and wants power+size THEN PHAT-style.
4. IF days/week = 6 AND can recover (good sleep, intermediate+) THEN PPL x2; IF very advanced, long-session capable, high volume tolerance THEN Arnold; otherwise cap 5 days.
5. IF person wants body-part split THEN use it but add indirect or a second hit on lagging muscles (e.g. Chest/back, shoulders/arms) so every muscle reaches ≥2 hits/wk if volume >12 sets.
6. IF weekly sets per muscle ≤10 THEN 1x/week acceptable [S5]; IF >10–12 THEN split across ≥2 sessions.
7. IF beginner THEN full body 2–3x/week; do not prescribe bro/Arnold/PHAT.
8. IF the person's schedule is irregular (shift work, travel, kids) THEN use rolling full body or rolling UL; train next workout in sequence regardless of weekday. IF schedule is stable THEN fixed-calendar.
9. IF one session missed THEN skip or resume sequence; do not double up. IF the missed session is the only one for a muscle this week and only 1–2 days remain THEN shift the session earlier into the next available day, dropping the lowest-priority day (accessories) instead.
10. IF ≥1 week missed THEN resume at the planned week, ~90–95% loads for first session if rusty; IF 3–4+ weeks THEN reduce loads ~10% and ramp back up over 1–2 weeks (practitioner synthesis grounded in [S15][S16]).
11. IF a split has two similar sessions back-to-back (e.g. Push→Arnold shoulders) THEN reorder or insert rest so the same muscle isn't hit on consecutive days at high volume (ACSM ~48 h) [S11].
12. IF time per session <45 min THEN prefer more days with fewer sets (FB/UL) rather than long body-part sessions.
13. IF powerlifter THEN schedule squat 2x, bench 2–3x, deadlift 1–2x per week; IF deadlift fatigue is high THEN deadlift once/week and use variation or lighter second hit [S13][S14].
14. IF someone insists on specialization THEN keep non-priority muscles at ≥4–8 sets/week (maintenance), add 3–6 sets for the priority muscle, up to ~3 hits/wk; limit to 1–2 priority muscles at a time.
15. IF the person has strong preferences for a split THEN honor it if weekly sets & frequency are acceptable (evidence supports flexibility [S2]).

## Common mistakes
- Treating the split label as the cause of results; ignoring weekly sets.
- Programming 6-day PPL for a beginner or low-recovery person.
- Body-part split for someone who misses days; one missed Monday = chest untrained for 2 weeks.
- Upper day with chest/back/shoulders/arms all at high volume (and no arm quality).
- Doubling sessions to "make up" missed days.
- Deadlift and heavy squat on adjacent days with no recovery.
- Counting 12 sets/muscle in one session instead of spreading.
- Forgetting rest days between sessions that hit same muscles (esp. FB 3 days on consecutive days).
- Over-trusting named programs' exact volumes; they are secondary-source summaries and vary.

## Controversies & open questions
- **Frequency for hypertrophy**: 2016 meta-analysis favored ≥2x [S1]; 2019 volume-equated analysis found no difference [S2]. Reconciliation: volume confounded; high frequency is a tool for volume. Remaining uncertainty: very high volume, advanced lifters, long durations.
- **Full body vs split in trained lifters**: handful of small trials with mixed findings, some favoring full body slightly [S8][S9]; no long-term trials.
- **Strength and frequency**: Nuckols' study favors 6/wk for squat/bench [S13], later meta-analyses show no volume-equated effect [S3][S4][S5]; individual recovery likely moderates.
- **Per-session volume limits**: preprint evidence only [S7]; "junk volume" thresholds are practitioner estimates.
- **Named-split definitions** differ across sources (PHAT/PHUL confusion, Arnold's actual routine unauthenticated [S17][S18]).
- Not findable: peer-reviewed comparison of PPL vs UL, torso/limbs or anterior/posterior; empirical rolling vs fixed-calendar adherence trials. Marked Practitioner.
