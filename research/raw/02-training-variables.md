# Resistance Training Variables & Dose-Response

> Research agent output · Topic 02 · 2026-10-08

## Executive summary

- **Weekly hard sets per muscle is the master dial.** More sets → more hypertrophy and strength, with diminishing returns (steeper for strength than hypertrophy). Default hypertrophy target: **10–20 fractional sets/muscle/week**; minimum useful ≈ 4–6; strength needs far less (≈ 2–3 hard sets per exercise per session, plateau early). [S1][S2][S16] `Strong` (direction), `Moderate` (exact numbers)
- **Count sets fractionally:** direct set = 1, indirect (synergist) set = 0.5 predicted outcomes best in the largest meta-regression. [S2] `Moderate`
- **Effort:** train hard but not necessarily to failure. Hypertrophy is best when sets end close to failure (≈ 0–3 RIR); strength gain is much less sensitive to RIR and depends more on heavy load (≥ 80% 1RM). Default 1–3 RIR; last set of an isolation exercise can go to failure. [S4][S5][S16] `Moderate`
- **Load/rep range:** hypertrophy occurs across ~30–100% 1RM (≈ 5–30+ reps) *if sets are hard*. Heavy loads (≥ 80–85% 1RM, 1–6 reps) are still best for maximal strength. The classic "repetition continuum" is a rough default, not a law. [S7][S8][S16] `Strong` (hypertrophy), `Moderate` (strength)
- **Frequency:** when weekly volume is equal, frequency barely affects hypertrophy; it helps strength (≥ 2 sessions/muscle/week) and is mostly a tool to distribute volume. Default: 2×/muscle/week. [S2][S6][S16] `Moderate`
- **Rest:** > 60 s is fine; ~2–3 min for compounds, 60–120 s for isolations. Beyond ~90 s hypertrophy differences are not detectable. Strength favors ≥ 2–3 min. [S9][S10][S11] `Moderate`
- **Tempo/ROM:** reps of 0.5–8 s give similar hypertrophy; avoid > 10 s reps. Use full ROM by default; lengthened-position partials are a viable (possibly superior for hypertrophy) option. [S12][S13] `Moderate` / `Contested`
- **Exercise selection:** compound vs isolation, free weights vs machines are roughly equal for hypertrophy when volume/effort match; choose by joint comfort, fatigue and coverage of every muscle. Do the priority lift first. [S14][S20][S22] `Moderate`
- **Advanced techniques (drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps, supersets, cluster sets)** do not beat straight sets per set but can save 30–50% of session time at equal volume. Use for efficiency, not because they are "better". [S17][S18][S19] `Moderate`

## Core concepts

**Generic → specific.**

1. **Stimulus vs fatigue.** Muscle growth/strength come from mechanical tension delivered by *hard sets* (sets close to failure with enough load to recruit high-threshold motor units). Programming variables are knobs on how many hard sets are done, how heavy, how often, and how recoverable.
2. **Volume** = hard working sets per muscle per week (not reps × load "tonnage"). Variants: *direct sets* (muscle is the prime mover, e.g. bench → chest), *indirect sets* (muscle is a synergist, e.g. bench → triceps/front delts). *Fractional counting* gives indirect sets weight 0.5 (some practitioners use 0.5 for synergists that are heavily loaded, 0 for minor). [S2][S3]
3. **Volume landmarks (Renaissance Periodization practitioner framework)** [S23] `Practitioner`:
   - **MV** maintenance volume (hold size; roughly ≈ 4–6 sets/wk for most muscles, ~⅓ of MEV).
   - **MEV** minimum effective volume (lowest to make progress; ≈ 6–10 sets/wk).
   - **MAV** maximum adaptive volume (best return per set; ≈ 10–20).
   - **MRV** maximum recoverable volume (more than this causes net regression; ≈ 16–25, highly individual).
   The landmarks are heuristics derived from experience + the dose-response literature, not research thresholds. Muscles differ (e.g. quads/hamstrings lower tolerance per set than biceps/calves/delts in RP tables). [S23]
4. **Intensity** has three languages: **%1RM** (load relative to max), **RIR** (reps in reserve: sets left before failure), **RPE** (rate of perceived exertion; RPE 10 = failure, RPE 9 ≈ 1 RIR, RPE 8 ≈ 2 RIR). RIR accuracy is poor far from failure (> 4–5 reps) and improves close to it; novices underestimate reps left. [S5][S16]
5. **Proximity to failure** and **load** are partially independent: a light load near failure and a heavy load far from failure can both be "hard"; a light load far from failure is not.
6. **Repetition continuum** (older model): ~1–5 reps/80–100% for strength, 6–12/60–80% hypertrophy, 15+/<60% endurance. Current view: hypertrophy is load-agnostic when near failure; strength is load-specific (heavy is better); endurance responds to light-load high-rep. [S8]
7. **Mechanisms behind secondary variables:** rest (recovery of reps → volume load), tempo (time under tension has little independent effect once near failure), ROM/muscle length (stretch-mediated hypertrophy at long muscle lengths), exercise order (earlier = fresher = more reps/load for that exercise).

## Key findings

1. **Volume dose-response, hypertrophy.** Schoenfeld 2017 meta-regression (15 studies, 34 groups): each additional weekly set ≈ +0.37% muscle gain; ≥ 10 sets/muscle/wk showed more growth than < 10 (≈ +3.9% difference high vs low); few trained subjects, little data above 10. [S1] `Moderate`
2. **Newer, larger volume meta-regression (Pelland et al.; 67 studies, 2,058 participants).** More weekly sets → more hypertrophy and strength (probability slope > 0 ≈ 100%) with diminishing returns; strength flattens much sooner than hypertrophy; hypertrophy has no clear plateau in the data range. Fractional set counting (indirect = 0.5) fit best. Preprint 2024; reported published in *Sports Medicine* late 2025 (exact effect sizes not verified here). [S2] `Moderate`
3. **Per-session volume (Remmert/Pelland preprint).** As secondarily reported: hypertrophy gains continue to ≈ 11 fractional sets/muscle/session with small increments; strength plateaus after ≈ 2 direct sets/muscle/session — suggesting splitting weekly volume over more sessions. Numbers from press/blog coverage of a non-peer-reviewed preprint, not independently verified. [S3] `Contested` (low confidence)
4. **2026 ACSM resistance-training prescription document (overview of 137 systematic reviews).** Hypertrophy: ≥ 10 sets/muscle/week with a dose-response (discussion: diminishing returns ≈ 18–20 weekly sets); strength: 2–3 sets per exercise per session, plateau ≈ 2–3 sets; ≥ 2 sessions/week; strength load ≥ 80% 1RM; hypertrophy 30–100% 1RM all work; failure not needed; power 30–70% 1RM, low-moderate volume. Its "quality of evidence" scores are its own, not GRADE. [S16] `Strong`-ish (synthesis of reviews) but newer and scored by its own method
5. **Proximity to failure.** Refalo 2023 (15 studies): training to failure vs not → trivial hypertrophy advantage (ES ≈ 0.19, not clearly significant), no evidence failure is superior; relationship may be non-linear. [S4] Robinson 2024 meta-regressions (RIR as continuous predictor): hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure; strength gain largely insensitive to RIR (load matters more); RIR values were *estimated* from study descriptions. [S5] `Moderate`
6. **Frequency.** Schoenfeld 2016: training a muscle 2×/wk > 1×/wk for hypertrophy (volume-equated), 3× vs 2× unclear. [S6] Schoenfeld 2019 (25 studies, 836 participants; via secondary summaries): no meaningful hypertrophy effect of frequency when volume equated; higher frequency helps when volume not equated. Pelland: hypertrophy–frequency slope negligible, strength–frequency positive with diminishing returns. ACSM 2026: ≥ 2 sessions/week for strength; 1 vs > 5 days no difference for hypertrophy when volume equated. [S2][S6][S16] `Moderate`
7. **Load and hypertrophy.** Schoenfeld 2017 (21 studies, 630 subjects): low-load (≤ 60% 1RM) to failure ≈ high-load (> 60%) for hypertrophy; high load superior for 1RM strength. [S7] 2021 re-examination: the repetition continuum is not supported for hypertrophy; adaptations are obtained across a wide range if effort is high; strength still favors heavy loads; endurance outcomes mixed. [S8] `Strong` (hypertrophy), `Moderate` (strength)
8. **Rest intervals.** Singer/Schoenfeld 2024 (Bayesian meta-analysis, 9 studies, 19 measures): short (≤ 60 s) SMD 0.48 vs longer (> 60 s) 0.56; small benefit of > 60 s (thigh P=0.88 favors longer, arm 0.74), whole-body ≈ 0; no appreciable difference beyond ~90 s; suggests NSCA's 30–90 s advice should be revisited. [S9] Schoenfeld 2016 RCT in trained men: 3 min > 1 min rest for muscle thickness (quads) and squat/bench 1RM (single study, ~21–23 men, 8 wks). [S10] Grgic 2018 systematic review: longer rests (≥ 2–3 min) favor strength. [S11] `Moderate`. ACSM 2026 found no strength difference between < 1 vs > 1 min. [S16] → partly `Contested`.
9. **Repetition duration.** Schoenfeld 2015 (8 studies): hypertrophy similar for 0.5–8 s per rep; > 10 s per rep inferior (few studies). [S12] ACSM 2026: fast (0.5 s) vs slow (8 s) no hypertrophy difference; eccentric-emphasis outperformed concentric-only. [S16] `Moderate`
10. **Range of motion.** Wolf 2023 meta-analysis (Partial vs full ROM): overall trivial edge to full ROM for hypertrophy (ES ≈ 0.1); partials at *shortened* lengths inferior; partials at *lengthened* positions equal or superior to full ROM (reported SMD ≈ 0.28 favoring long-length partials; not verified). [S13] Follow-up RCTs (Pedrosa 2022/23) support lengthened partials. `Moderate`; for strength use full ROM (ACSM 2026, 50% quality score). [S16]
11. **Exercise order.** Nunes 2021 meta-analysis (11 studies): exercises placed first gain more strength; hypertrophy about equal regardless of order. [S14] ACSM 2009/2026: large before small, multi-joint before single-joint, strength exercise first. [S15][S16] `Moderate`
12. **Compound vs isolation.** 2023 meta-analysis (7 RCTs, 10 comparisons) of single- vs multi-joint exercise: similar whole-muscle hypertrophy; single-joint may help target individual heads/regions (hypothesis). [S20] `Moderate`
13. **Free weights vs machines.** Similar strength and hypertrophy when volume/effort matched; specificity may favor free weights for the free-weight test lift. [S16][S22] `Moderate` (S22 is practitioner summary of the literature)
14. **Exercise variation.** Baz-Valle 2019 RCT (21 resistance-trained men, 8 wks): varying exercises every session vs fixed → same hypertrophy/strength, higher motivation. Rotating exercises across mesocycles is fine; random every-session variation not needed. [S21] `Moderate` (single small RCT)
15. **Drop sets.** Coleman et al. meta-analysis: trivial effect favoring drop sets (hypertrophy ES 0.08; strength 0.07) but sessions took ~½–⅓ the time at similar volume. [S17] `Moderate`
16. **Rest-pause / myo-reps.** Only low-quality direct evidence; rest-pause may give a modest hypertrophy edge in one 2026 review (unverified); myo-reps have no controlled studies — extrapolate from rest-pause/short-rest sets. `Practitioner` [S17][S23]
17. **Supersets.** Meta-analysis of supersets vs traditional sets: similar hypertrophy, strength; sessions ≈ 36–50% shorter; agonist-antagonist pairs maintain reps (SMD +0.68) while same-muscle pairs lower volume load (SMD −1.08). [S18] `Moderate`
18. **Cluster sets.** No difference vs traditional for strength (ES −0.05) or hypertrophy (ES −0.05); mainly reduce fatigue (useful for power/velocity). ACSM 2026: cluster/complex/rest-redistribution no benefit. [S16][S19] `Moderate`

## Decision rules

**Volume**
- IF goal = hypertrophy AND beginner THEN start ≈ 6–10 fractional sets/muscle/wk; add 1–2 sets/wk when progress stalls and recovery is good.
- IF goal = hypertrophy AND intermediate/advanced THEN 10–20 sets/muscle/wk; start near the low end (MEV ≈ 8–10) and add ~1 set per muscle per week across a 4–6-week block, then deload.
- IF a muscle is a priority/lagging THEN raise it toward 16–22 sets/wk and drop others to ~6–10 (maintenance/MEV).
- IF goal = strength THEN lower per-muscle weekly volume is enough (≈ 2–3 hard sets per exercise per session, 4–10 per muscle/wk) and spend effort on specific heavy practice of the target lifts; adding sets beyond 3/exercise/session gives little. [S2][S3][S16]
- IF goal = maintenance (time-poor) THEN 4–6 sets/muscle/wk at ≥ 1–3 RIR, 1–2×/wk.
- IF performance falls for ≥ 2 sessions, joints ache, or sleep/appetite worsen THEN cut volume ~30–50% for a week (deload) before changing anything else; if recurring each block, reduce MRV estimate.
- IF a session would exceed ≈ 8–11 fractional sets for one muscle THEN split across 2 sessions (diminishing returns per session). [S3] (low confidence)
- IF counting sets for muscle X THEN count direct = 1, indirect = 0.5. Don't double-count both pressing and fly work as chest-only "free" sets for triceps.

**Intensity / proximity to failure**
- IF hypertrophy THEN end most working sets at 1–3 RIR; use 0–1 RIR on isolation/last sets; avoid failure on heavy barbell compounds (axial fatigue, form breakdown). [S4][S5]
- IF strength THEN work mostly at 80–92% (RPE 7–9), peak singles/doubles occasionally at RPE 9–10; don't go to failure on main lifts. [S5][S16]
- IF user is a beginner THEN use 2–4 RIR (they underestimate effort) and let load rise linearly; add effort cues ("stop when form breaks / last rep slows").
- IF user has little RIR experience THEN use rep targets with a "2-for-2" rule (if you exceed the top of the rep range by 2 on the last set, add load next time).
- IF equipment limits loads (e.g., dumbbells ≤ 25 kg) THEN go to 0–2 RIR with higher reps (20–30) — low load works if sets are near failure. [S7][S8]

**Rep ranges**
- IF the exercise is a heavy barbell compound (squat, deadlift, bench, press, row) THEN 5–10 reps (strength end 3–6).
- IF the exercise is isolation, machine or cable THEN 10–20 reps (up to 30 for calves/delts/arms if joint-friendly).
- IF user has joint pain with heavy loading THEN use 12–25 reps at 1–2 RIR; hypertrophy remains similar. [S7]
- IF goal = endurance THEN 15–30+ reps, shorter rest (30–90 s), but still include some moderate-load work.
- IF goal = power THEN 1–5 reps at 30–70% (or ≥ 75–90% for heavy-power lifts) with maximal intent, stop at ≤ 10–20% velocity/height drop, rest 2–5 min. [S16]

**Frequency**
- IF goal = hypertrophy THEN train each muscle 2×/week (1–3× all fine at equal volume); use more days only to cap per-session sets. [S2][S6]
- IF goal = strength THEN practice main lifts ≥ 2×/week (typically 3–4×) with low per-session sets. [S2][S16]
- IF days/week ≤ 2 THEN full-body each session; volume per muscle must still hit ≥ 4–6 sets/wk.
- IF only 1 session per muscle per week is possible THEN keep it but raise sets to 8–12 in that session and expect slightly less efficient hypertrophy; no more than ~10–11 fractional sets/session. [S3]

**Rest**
- IF compound, heavy or strength/power goal THEN rest 2–5 min.
- IF hypertrophy compound THEN 2–3 min (≥ 90 s); IF isolation THEN 60–90 s.
- IF time-constrained THEN shorten rest only on isolation work or use antagonist supersets; keep compound rest ≥ 90 s. [S9][S18]
- IF reps drop > 30% from set to set due to short rest THEN lengthen rest or reduce sets/ load; volume load is the real driver. [S9]

**Tempo / ROM**
- IF no special need THEN 1–2 s concentric, 2–3 s eccentric, no pause; do not prescribe very slow (> 8–10 s) reps. [S12]
- IF the exercise stretches the muscle under load (RDL, incline curl, overhead triceps extension, deep squat/Bulgarian split squat, cable fly) THEN use full ROM with a controlled stretch; optionally finish a set with 3–8 lengthened partials. [S13]
- IF strength/competition lift THEN use the competition ROM and pause style.
- IF joint limits the bottom range THEN use the full range you can control; do not bounce off the stretch.
- IF user wants sport/functional carryover THEN prefer ROM used in the sport.

**Exercise selection**
- IF building any plan THEN cover movement patterns weekly: squat/knee-dominant, hinge, horizontal push, vertical push, horizontal pull, vertical pull, lunge/single-leg, plus calves, core, arms as needed. [S16]
- IF the muscle's regions (heads) matter (triceps long head, rectus femoris, upper chest) THEN add 1 isolation exercise that loads the region at long length (overhead extension, leg extension, incline press). [S20] (hypothesis)
- IF beginners or low skill/time THEN favor stable machines and simple compounds; free weights are not necessary. [S22]
- IF injury/pain on an exercise THEN substitute with a similar-pattern machine/dumbbell option; keep weekly sets constant.
- IF a muscle needs more volume but systemic fatigue is high THEN add isolation/machine sets rather than more squats/deadlifts.
- IF plan lasts > 4 weeks THEN keep main exercises constant and progress them; rotate accessory exercises every ~4–8 weeks or when stale/painful (variation mainly helps motivation). [S21]

**Exercise order**
- IF a lift/muscle is a priority THEN do it first (most rested). [S14]
- IF strength goal THEN order: power/technical → main heavy compound → secondary compound → accessories.
- IF hypertrophy of lagging muscle THEN place it earlier; pre-exhaust is unnecessary. [S14]
- IF antagonist supersets are used THEN the order within pair doesn't matter much; the heavy lift still leads.

**Advanced techniques**
- IF time is the constraint THEN replace straight sets with antagonist supersets (e.g., row + press) or drop sets on the last isolation set; expect similar hypertrophy/strength. [S17][S18]
- IF time is not a constraint THEN default to straight sets (best-supported, easiest to progress).
- IF using drop sets/rest-pause/myo-reps THEN restrict to isolation/machine exercises, ≤ 1–2 per muscle per session, and count the extra reps as ~1–2 additional effective sets, not a whole set each; avoid with heavy barbell compounds and for beginners.
- IF user wants fatigue-reduced power or heavy-speed work THEN cluster sets are reasonable; otherwise no advantage. [S16][S19]
- IF any technique raises soreness enough to reduce next-session performance THEN remove it.

**Default per-goal stack (when no other info)**
- Hypertrophy: 2×/wk/muscle, 12–16 sets/wk, 6–20 reps, 1–2 RIR, 2-min rest on compounds/60–90 s on isolation, full ROM + lengthened emphasis, order priority-first.
- Strength: main lift 3×/wk, 3–5 sets of 3–6 reps at 80–90% (RPE 7–9), 3–5 min rest, accessories 8–12 reps; weekly accessory sets 6–10 per supporting muscle.
- Endurance: 2–3×/wk, 2–3 sets of 15–25 reps at 60% or lower, 30–60 s rest.
- Power: 2×/wk, 3–6 sets of 2–5 explosive reps at 30–70%, full rest, before strength work.

## Common mistakes

- Prescribing 25–30+ sets/muscle/week to beginners, or < 6 sets/week for hypertrophy.
- Counting sets as "exercises" instead of *hard working sets*; ignoring warm-ups vs working sets.
- Ignoring indirect volume (training bench, OHP and dips 3×/wk and then adding 12 triceps sets).
- Prescribing every set to failure (or never near failure — "RPE 5" sets).
- Insisting hypertrophy requires 8–12 reps only, or that "light weights don't count".
- Long strict rest cuts (30–45 s) for compounds, which lower volume load.
- Super-slow tempos (≥ 10 s reps) or arbitrary time-under-tension targets.
- Overloading session volume for one muscle (> 10–12 sets) instead of spreading across days.
- Randomizing exercises every session (loss of progressive overload tracking) or never changing a painful exercise.
- Putting accessory/isolation work before the main compound lift for a strength goal.
- Treating RP landmarks as fixed laws; MRV varies by person, muscle, sleep and diet.
- Using drop sets/rest-pause on every set, creating junk fatigue without more growth.

## Controversies & open questions

- **Exact volume ceiling** for hypertrophy: data show diminishing returns near 18–20 sets (ACSM 2026) but no plateau in Pelland; trained lifters' data sparse; individual MRV differs. `Contested`
- **Fractional vs full vs direct-only counting:** fractional won statistically but exact weights per muscle/exercise are unknown; practitioners also use "synergists count 0.5 for major pushers only". [S2]
- **Per-session volume caps** (≈ 11 fractional sets hypertrophy; ≈ 2 sets strength) come from a preprint; replication needed. [S3]
- **Proximity to failure:** Refalo 2023 (no clear advantage of failure) vs Robinson 2024 (closer to failure better for hypertrophy, nonlinear). RIR estimates are noisy; whether 0 vs 3 RIR matters for trained individuals at equal volume is unclear. [S4][S5]
- **Rest intervals:** meta-analysis favors > 60 s for hypertrophy by a small margin; ACSM 2026 found no strength difference between < 1 and > 1 min; single RCT (3 vs 1 min) favored longer rests; wide heterogeneity. [S9][S10][S16]
- **Lengthened partials / stretch-mediated hypertrophy:** promising, but based on small short trials and mostly lower body or single-site outcomes; effect sizes differ across reviews. [S13]
- **Tempo:** slow eccentrics may help (ACSM 2026 reports eccentric emphasis > concentric-only), but no tempo for each phase is firmly established.
- **Frequency for hypertrophy:** equal volume → no difference, yet higher frequency makes high volume easier to sustain; trained-population data limited. [S2][S6]
- **Myo-reps and rest-pause:** essentially no direct controlled trials for myo-reps; recommendations extrapolated.
- **Power/endurance prescriptions** are better supported by consensus textbooks than by modern meta-regressions; this note gives only moderate-confidence defaults.
- **Publication note:** the Pelland meta-regression and Remmert per-session analysis were retrieved as preprints/abstract; effect-size tables were not read in full.
