# Verification: Resistance-Training Numbers

> Research agent output · Topic 12 · 2026-10-08

## Summary

- **ACSM 2026 stand exists and was read in full** (Europe PMC full-text XML, PMC12965823). Correct citation: **Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MA, Lowisz CV, Rawson ES, Schoenfeld BJ, Smith-Ryan AE, Steen JP, Thomas GA, Triplett NT, Washington TA, Werner TJ, Phillips SM. *ACSM Position Stand: Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews.* Med Sci Sports Exerc 58(4):851–872, April 2026. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897, PMID 41843416.** Currier is first author; Phillips is senior/corresponding. File 03 ("Phillips et al.") is wrong on first author; file 10 is right. Files 01/03 are wrong that it is unretrievable; the full text is open access (CC BY-NC-ND).
- Most of file 10's ACSM specifics are **confirmed**, with three corrections. (a) "2–3 RIR" is a hedged proposal. The stand says there is "insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR … targets". (b) "Training experience had minimal impact" is a carried-over finding from the authors' own 2023 network meta-analysis. This overview did not test it, and most included evidence is from novices. (c) ACSM gives **no inter-set rest recommendation** (rest = "insufficient data"). File 10's "3–5 min rest" is from the 2009 stand.
- **Pelland et al. is peer-reviewed.** *Sports Med* 56(2):481–505, online 4 Dec 2025 (issue dated 2026). Files 01/02 treat it as a preprint, which is outdated. The per-session paper (Remmert/Pelland, "Is There Too Much of a Good Thing?", SportRxiv 10.51224/srxiv.537, April 2025) is **still a preprint** as far as Crossref/Europe PMC show. The ~2 direct (strength) and ~11 fractional (hypertrophy) sets are confirmed as stated in its abstract. But the number is a "point of undetectable outcome superiority" (PUOS), **not a ceiling**. The authors say it "should be interpreted cautiously" because high per-session volumes are sparse.
- Detail in Pelland's full text (Springer paywall) could not be read. Only the abstract was read. The "~1 set/wk strength, ~4 sets/wk hypertrophy minimum" figures in file 10 are **Unverifiable** and appear nowhere in the abstract.
- Other headline numbers (Schoenfeld 2016/2019, Grgic 2018, Refalo 2023, Robinson 2024, Schoenfeld 2017 load, Singer 2024, Schoenfeld 2016 rest, Spiering 2021, Bickel 2011, Moesgaard 2022, Coleman 2024, Roberts 2020, Bosquet 2013) all check out against abstracts. Corrections are mostly nuance: "volume-equated" labelling, Moesgaard/Williams attribution, and the "retraining = ½ layoff" figure, which is a blog estimate, not a measured result.
- File 07 line 59 cites "a second meta-analysis (35 studies) ES 0.31" while its research log says Moesgaard was "not retrieved". That is Moesgaard 2022 and the number is correct.

## Resolutions

Single recommended defaults for the wiki. Grades are for the *default*, not each underlying study.

### Rest intervals (one rule)
**Rest long enough to repeat the target reps on the next set. Default 2–3 min for multi-joint lifts, 1–2 min for isolation/single-joint lifts. Never default below 60 s.**

| Situation | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy compound (≥80% 1RM or ≤6 reps), strength goal | **3 min** | 2–5 min (use ≥3 for trained lifters) |
| Compound for hypertrophy (6–15 reps) | **2 min** | 90 s–3 min |
| Isolation / machine | **90 s** | 60–120 s |
| Time-poor | 60–90 s, compensate by adding sets or accept fewer reps | do not go below 60 s for compounds |
| Untrained / beginner | 90–120 s | 60–120 s |

Basis: Singer 2024 (>60 s beats ≤60 s slightly; no appreciable benefit >90 s) [S15]; Schoenfeld 2016 RCT (3 > 1 min, trained) [S16]; Grgic 2018 (>2 min for strength in trained, 60–120 s in untrained) [S17]; Rosa 2023 (2–3 min give similar rep performance) [S32]. **ACSM 2026 gives no rest guidance** [S1]. Grade: Moderate for hypertrophy (≥90 s), Moderate for strength (≥2 min), Practitioner for the compound/isolation split.

### Per-session volume cap (one default)
**Soft cap: 10 fractional hard sets per muscle per session (≈ 6–8 direct sets in practice). If the weekly target needs more, add another session for that muscle rather than extending the session.**
- Strength work on one lift: **2–3 hard sets per exercise** (ACSM 2026 [S1]; PUOS ≈ 2 direct sets [S5]); up to 5 for a specific peak or technique block.
- Why 10 and not 4 or 11: ~11 fractional is the preprint PUOS for hypertrophy [S5], meaning extra sets add little detectable benefit, not harm. Authors say it is uncertain at high volumes. 4–6 per muscle/session is a practical floor with no research support as a cap. 6–10 direct has no primary source. Choosing 10 sits under the only quantified point (11) and matches ACSM's weekly ≥10.
- Grade: Contested (preprint). Weekly volume matters more than session distribution once frequency ≥2 [S1][S3][S8].

### Default RIR
**Default 2 RIR (RPE 8) for working sets; allowed range 1–3. Final set of an isolation exercise may go to 0–1 RIR. Beginners, older adults and heavy barbell compounds: 3 RIR (RPE 7).**
- Basis: ACSM 2026 suggests "near-failure or 2–3 RIR" and finds failure unnecessary [S1]. Robinson 2024: hypertrophy improves slightly as sets end closer to failure, strength unaffected [S11]. Refalo 2023/2024: no advantage of failure [S10][S12]. 2 RIR is the compromise: near enough for the hypertrophy gradient, far enough to protect recovery and form.
- Grade: Moderate for "2–3 RIR is adequate; failure not required". Experience-specific RIR values: Practitioner.
- RIR is estimated, and estimates are noisy, especially for beginners (Robinson notes RIR was estimated from descriptions) [S11].

### Weekly set ranges per goal (hard sets per muscle per week; fractional counting, indirect = 0.5)

| Goal / population | Default | Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy, intermediate | **12** | 10–20 | ACSM ≥10 [S1]; diminishing returns ~18–20 [S1][S3] |
| Hypertrophy, beginner | **8** | 6–10 | Practitioner; ACSM says novices benefit from "various RT programs" [S1] |
| Hypertrophy, advanced / specialising a muscle | **16** | 12–20 (up to ~22 short-term for a priority muscle) | Practitioner; no plateau in Pelland [S3] |
| Maximal strength (lift-specific muscles) | **6** (≈ 2–3 sets × 2–3 sessions) | 4–10 | ACSM 2–3 sets/exercise, ≥2 d/wk [S1]; strong diminishing returns [S3] |
| Maintenance (time-poor) | **4–6**, heavy-ish loads, 1–2 sessions/wk | 3–6 | Spiering 2021 (1 set/exercise, 1 session/wk preserved strength/size in young for 32 wk if intensity maintained) [S20]; older: 2 sessions, 2–3 sets [S20]; Bickel [S19] |
| Muscle endurance / power | per ACSM: power ≤24 reps × sets per exercise (30–70% 1RM) | 3–9 power sets | ACSM [S1] (Practitioner for endurance) |
| Minimum useful (any goal, general fitness) | **≥1 hard set per major pattern, ≥2 d/wk** ("minimal dose") | — | Androulakis-Korakakis [S18]; Nuzzo [S21]; ACSM [S1] |

Frequency default: **2 sessions/muscle/wk** (hypertrophy is frequency-insensitive when volume equated [S8][S1]; strength benefits from ≥2 [S1][S9]).
