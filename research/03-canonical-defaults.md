## Progression & periodization defaults

- **Default progression model:** double progression (rep range + target RIR; add load when top of range is hit at target RIR). Beginners on main barbell lifts: linear progression per session (+2.5 kg / 5 lb upper, +5 kg / 10 lb lower), with a defined reset on failure.
- **Mesocycle:** 4–8 weeks (default 5 working + 1 deload), volume ramps (~+1 set/muscle/week), RIR falls (3 → 1). Practitioner.
- **Deload:** ~1 week, cut volume 30–50%, keep load mostly; every 4–8 weeks of hard training or on fatigue signals. Practitioner (Coleman 2024 RCT, n=39: a full rest week did not affect hypertrophy; continuous training gave slightly better lower-body strength).
- **Periodization:** periodized > non-periodized for 1RM strength (Moesgaard 2022 ES 0.31); no difference for hypertrophy; undulating > linear for 1RM only in trained lifters. The specific model matters less than having progression.
- **Plateau:** no progress on a priority lift for 3–4+ weeks with sleep/nutrition consistent → check fatigue, volume, technique before changing program.
- **Layoffs:** "regain in about half the layoff time" is a **practitioner estimate**, not a measured result. Restart at reduced loads and ramp over a few weeks.

## Conditioning & hybrid defaults

- **Health floor (WHO 2020 / US PAG 2018):** 150–300 min/wk moderate OR 75–150 min/wk vigorous aerobic (1 vigorous min ≈ 2 moderate) + muscle strengthening of all major groups ≥2 d/wk. Older adults: multicomponent (balance + strength) ≥3 d/wk **(WHO 2020; PAG gives no day count)**.
- **Intensity distribution:** mostly easy (talk-test/RPE 3–4) + 1–2 hard sessions/wk. The ~80/20 polarized edge (Oliveira 2024, SMD 0.24 VO2peak) applies only to **highly trained athletes in <12-week interventions**; for recreational people any sensible distribution works.
- **HIIT vs MICT:** similar or better VO2max gains in less time. Fat loss: **small and inconsistent difference** (Wewege 2017: none; Viana 2019: ~28.5% more absolute fat loss with intervals). Time-efficiency is the real HIIT advantage.
- **Zone 2:** good for low-fatigue volume; **not** shown to be uniquely superior. Contested.
- **10% rule:** no injury benefit in RCT (Buist 2008). Soft ceiling only.
- **Interference (Schumann 2022):** max strength SMD −0.06, hypertrophy −0.01 (negligible); explosive strength −0.28. **Only moderator: same-session / <3 h gap, and only for explosive strength.** Training status did *not* moderate. Huiberts: male lower-body strength more affected (SMD −0.43; female data sparse).
- **Sequencing:** same session → lift first if strength is the priority (Murlasits: ~+4 kg lower-body 1RM). Spacing: ≥6 h apart or separate days; ≥24 h before key quality sessions (Robineau).
- **Lifters' cardio:** 2–3 sessions/wk is safe; prefer cycling/rowing/incline walking; keep hard intervals away from heavy leg days.
- Wilson 2012 duration/frequency thresholds (e.g. "50–60 vs 20–30 min/day") are **unverified**. Do not state them as facts.

## Goal blending

- One primary goal, at most 1–2 secondary per block.
- "Primary goal gets ~60–70% of weekly hard-set volume" is an **unsourced heuristic**. Present it as a rule of thumb (Practitioner), not evidence.

## Individualization

- Experience is classified by **how fast progress happens**, not years: beginner = progress session to session; intermediate = week to week; advanced = month to month or block to block.
- ACSM 2026's "training experience had minimal impact" comes from the authors' 2023 network meta-analysis, mostly in novices. Don't overstate it.
- Women: same programming principles; menstrual-cycle-based programming is **not supported**.
- Older adults: resistance training is the priority; add power (fast concentric, 40–70% 1RM) and balance; default 3 RIR, avoid routine failure.
- Bickel 2011: older adults kept *strength* at ⅓ dose but **not** muscle size.
- Pain: train around, not through; a conservative pain-monitoring rule is ≤3/10 during, settled by next day (Practitioner). Refer red flags to a professional. No medical treatment advice.
