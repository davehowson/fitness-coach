# Populations and Individualization

> Purpose: adjust a program's dose, complexity, exercise choice and progression to the person's experience, age, sex, time, equipment, injuries, layoff status and recovery.
> Use when: the intake shows anything other than "healthy adult, trained, full gym, plenty of time", or you must classify experience level.
> Depends on: [intake](02-intake.md), [variables](03-variables.md), [goals](04-goals.md), [progression](08-progression.md), [exercise library](12-exercise-library.md), [quality checklist](14-quality-checklist.md)

Scope: program-design guidance, not medical advice. For a medical condition the rule is "get clearance / refer", not "treat".

## Key rules (TL;DR)

1. Individualization changes **dose, complexity, exercise selection and progression speed**, not principles. Order: (1) safety screen → (2) constraints (time, equipment, injuries) → (3) experience level → (4) population modifiers → (5) recovery-based volume adjustment.
2. Classify experience by **rate of progress**, not years: beginner = progress session to session; intermediate = week to week; advanced = month to month or block to block. (Practitioner)
3. Hypertrophy weekly hard sets per muscle by level: **8 / 12 / 16** (ranges 6–10 / 10–20 / 12–20). Max strength **6** (4–10). Maintenance **4–6**. Minimum useful **≥1 hard set per major pattern, ≥2 d/wk**. Frequency default 2×/muscle/wk. Details: [variables](03-variables.md).
4. Default effort 2 RIR; **beginners, older adults and heavy barbell compounds 3 RIR**. Failure is not required; avoid routine failure in older adults. (Moderate) [1]
5. Women: same programming principles as men. **Menstrual-cycle-based programming is not supported**; symptom-based autoregulation is fine. (Moderate) [5][6]
6. Older adults: resistance training is the priority; add power (fast concentric, 40–70% 1RM) and balance; multicomponent activity ≥3 d/wk (WHO 2020; the US PAG gives no day count). (Strong guideline; power edge Moderate, low certainty) [3][4][7]
7. Time-poor: hit weekly **sets**, not weekly days. Frequency barely matters for hypertrophy when volume is equated. (Strong) [1]
8. Home/bodyweight: low-load work near failure builds similar muscle to heavy loads; strength gains are somewhat lower. Overload via leverage, tempo, unilateral work, ROM. (Moderate) [1][10]
9. Pain: **train around, not through.** Conservative rule: pain ≤3/10 during, not worsening, settled by next day (Practitioner). Refer red flags; give no diagnosis or treatment.
10. Layoff: restart at reduced loads and ramp over a few weeks. "Regain in about half the layoff time" is a **practitioner estimate**, not a measured result. [9][11]
11. Poor recovery (sleep, stress, deficit): cut volume and add RIR rather than stacking hard work on a depleted person. (Practitioner)
12. The ACSM 2026 numbers cover **healthy adults only**; do not copy them to obese, sarcopenic or frail clinical populations. [1]

## Experience levels

| Variable | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rate of progress | Load/reps up nearly every session | Up weekly to monthly | Up over months or blocks |
| Typical history (heuristic) | <~6–12 months consistent, or returning after ≥1 yr off | ~1–3 yr | Multi-year |
| Hypertrophy sets/muscle/wk | **8** (6–10) | **12** (10–20) | **16** (12–20; ≤~22 short-term for a priority muscle) |
| Sets/exercise (strength) | 2–3 | 2–4 | 2–5 (peaking/technique only) |
| Effort | **3 RIR** | **2 RIR** (1–3) | 2 RIR; selective 0–1 on last isolation sets |
| Rest | 90–120 s | Canonical rests ([variables](03-variables.md)) | Canonical rests; ≥3 min heavy compounds |
| Complexity / exercise choice | Machines, goblet/DB, simple barbell; 4–6 exercises/session repeated | Barbell + variations | Variations, specialization, joint-stress rotation |
| Progression | Linear per session on main barbell lifts (+2.5 kg/5 lb upper, +5 kg/10 lb lower), defined reset on failure | Double progression (rep range + target RIR) | Block/wave loading, deload every 4–8 wk or on fatigue signals |
| Days/wk | 2–3 full body | 3–4 | 4–5 (legacy ACSM 2009 staging; thin evidence base, 3 studies n=59, so treat as Practitioner, not a target) |

Grades: set counts Practitioner/Moderate (canonical); RIR by experience Practitioner (no source gives experience-specific RIR). [1][2]

Notes:
- Beginners improve with nearly any sensible program; most early gain is neural/skill. Keep it simple and repeat few exercises.
- Do not read "training experience had minimal impact" (ACSM 2026) as "trained lifters need the same dose". It is the authors' 2023 network meta-analysis finding about response to a prescription, and most synthesized evidence is from novices. [1]
- Moves up a level when linear progression stalls for ≥2–3 consecutive attempts at a lift; moves to advanced when main-lift progress is slower than monthly after >2–3 years consistent. (Practitioner)
- Unsure of level → default to intermediate-lite: ~10 sets/muscle/wk, 2–3 RIR.

## Older adults

| Group | Frequency | Resistance dose | Add | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50–64, healthy | 2–3 d/wk | 2–3 sets × 6–12, 3 RIR | 1–2 power sets, balance | Same core template as adults [1] |
| 65+ | Resistance 2–3 d/wk; multicomponent (balance + strength) **≥3 d/wk** (WHO) | 1–3 sets × 8–12, moderate load, 3 RIR | Power: 2–4 sets at 40–70% 1RM, fast concentric, controlled eccentric (leg press, chair rise, step-up); balance/coordination | Avoid routine failure and breath-holding maximal efforts; slower progression [1][3][4] |
| Frail / mobility-limited / fall history | Clinician-informed | Supported or seated, light | Fall-prevention | Refer for fall-risk assessment [3] |

- Power evidence: 20 RCTs, 566 older adults; physical function SMD 0.30, **low certainty**; no clear edge over traditional strength training for strength, mass or gait speed. Typical protocol 12 wk, 1–3 d/wk, 2–4 sets, 40–70%. (Moderate/low certainty) [7]
- Failure caution: ACSM 2026 notes failure may be inadvisable for older individuals (vascular and injury risk). (Moderate) [1]
- Bickel 2011: older adults kept **strength** at ⅓ of the training dose but **not muscle size**. Young adults kept both. Do not tell older users that ⅓ volume maintains muscle. (Moderate) [8]
- Spiering 2021 (narrative review): maintenance for older adults needs ~2 sessions/wk and 2–3 sets with relative load kept. (Moderate) [12]

## Women

- Hypertrophy: no sex difference in relative gains (ES 0.07); lower-body relative strength similar; upper-body relative strength gains favored women (ES −0.60, heterogeneous). 2025 Bayesian meta-analysis (PeerJ): absolute gain slightly favors males (SMD 0.19), relative gain equal, not influenced by experience. (Moderate) [5]
- Rules: same volume, frequency and load rules as men. Do not reduce load or restrict to "toning" rep ranges.
- Menstrual cycle: umbrella review of meta-analyses finds no reliable basis for phase-periodization; literature is low quality with poor phase verification. Only soreness markers showed trivial–small effects. Absence of evidence is not proof of no effect, so allow symptom-based autoregulation (reduce volume/intensity on days with severe symptoms). (Moderate) [6]
- **Pregnancy / postpartum safety note** (ACOG 2020, secondary read; Strong as professional guideline): uncomplicated pregnancy → exercise is safe and desirable; previously active women can continue; ~150 min/wk moderate aerobic plus light-moderate resistance; RPE/talk-test guided; avoid abdominal-trauma risk, contact/fall-risk activities, scuba, heavy Valsalva, prolonged supine; stop on warning signs and refer. Require clinician clearance before programming. Postpartum: resume gradually after clearance, start low; leakage, bulging, pelvic pain or pressure → refer to a pelvic-health professional. No pregnancy-specific resistance dosing was found in the research. [13]
- Huiberts: male lower-body strength was more affected by concurrent training (SMD −0.43; female 0.08); female data sparse. See [hybrid](10-hybrid.md). (Contested) [14]

## Youth (under 18)

- 2014 international consensus: supervised, technique-first resistance training is safe and beneficial. (Strong consensus) [15]
- Defaults (numbers are background knowledge, not retrieved; Practitioner): 2–3 non-consecutive days/wk, 1–3 sets × 6–15 reps, light–moderate loads, fundamental movement skill and play included. Qualified supervision required. No max testing, no ego lifting.
- Do not treat youth as small adults; do not ban resistance training on stunting myths.

## Time-poor

| Available time | Structure | Weekly sets reached | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~20–30 min, 1 d/wk | Full body, 5–6 compound/machine exercises, 1–2 hard sets each, 5–10 reps | ~1–4 per muscle | Meaningful strength gains; limited hypertrophy |
| 2 × 30 min | Full-body A/B, 4–5 exercises, 2 sets | ~4–8 | Good strength; moderate hypertrophy |
| 3 × 30–45 min | Full body, antagonist or non-competing supersets, 2 sets each | ~8–14 | Near-full results |
| Any (maintain only) | Key lifts, heavy loads | 4–6 per muscle, 1–2 sessions/wk | Maintenance |

- Levers: compound lifts, antagonist supersets (no outcome loss), rest 60–90 s on isolation (keep ~3 min on heavy compounds if strength is the goal; never <60 s), drop optional isolation. (Moderate)
- Evidence: all minimal-dose strategies raise strength in people not currently training; single-set and "weekend warrior" (one concentrated weekly session) are best supported. Trained men gained 1RM on 1 set, 1–3×/wk. ACSM 2026 says minimal doses give substantial gains but there is insufficient evidence for specific recommendations. The specific "~1 set/wk strength, ~4 sets/wk hypertrophy" thresholds are **unverified; do not state them as sourced**. (Moderate) [1][16][17]
- IF time shrinks temporarily THEN keep loads, cut sets to about ⅓, do not stop training. (Practitioner, from Spiering/Bickel direction) [8][12]
- Never write 6-day plans for time-poor users.

## Home and bodyweight

1. Pick the hardest variation allowing ≥8 reps; then 15–30 reps at 1–3 RIR; progress leverage before reps. (Moderate for near-failure low-load hypertrophy) [1][10]
2. Overload order: (1) closer to failure with higher reps, (2) slow eccentrics 3–4 s and pauses, (3) harder leverage (incline → flat → decline, two-leg → single-leg), (4) more ROM (deficit, elevated feet), (5) load via backpack/bands/water jugs, (6) shorter rest or density, (7) unilateral variants. (Practitioner)
3. IF >~30 reps become easy THEN advance the variation.
4. IF no pull-up bar THEN inverted/towel rows and band pulldowns; flag the missing vertical pull in the plan.
5. IF dumbbells only THEN unilateral lifts, slow eccentrics, 8–20 reps.
6. Fiber-level meta-analysis at failure: no significant difference low vs high load (very wide intervals). Whole-muscle hypertrophy similar, strength gains lower with low load. Push-up evidence is small studies in untrained men. (Moderate) [10]

Practitioner. Use it with the [exercise library](12-exercise-library.md): substitute within the same pattern, keep the muscle targets and set counts.

## Injury and pain: working around

Principles (Practitioner):
1. Train around, not through. Keep training uninjured regions.
2. A movement aggravates a joint → swap within the same pattern (change ROM, load path, grip, stance or machine) using the matrix above.
3. Pain-monitoring rule: ≤3/10 during, not worsening in-session, back to baseline by next morning; otherwise regress one level. Borrowed from tendon and patellofemoral rehab research; 5/10 maximum in that literature; not validated for general lifting or low-back pain. [18]
4. Note in the plan that it is general and to coordinate with the person's clinician for diagnosed injuries or chronic pain.
5. No diagnosis, no treatment, no rehab prescriptions.

**Red flags: stop the movement and refer to a physician/physiotherapist**
- Sharp, worsening or radiating pain; numbness, weakness or loss of bladder/bowel control; night pain; swelling; pain after trauma.
- Chest pain, unexplained dyspnea, syncope or dizziness on exertion.
- Known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease; uncontrolled hypertension; recent surgery; pregnancy complications.
→ Do not generate a hard program until cleared.

## Returning after a layoff

| Time off | Expected loss | Re-entry (Practitioner) |
|---|---|---|
| <2 weeks | Negligible | Resume normally |
| 2–4 weeks | Small (older adults earlier) | Week 1 at ~80–90% of last loads, then normal |
| 1–3 months | Moderate strength loss | Week 1 ≈ ⅓–½ of previous loads (same sets × reps), week 2 at 3–5 RIR; ramp over a few weeks |
| 3–12 months | Substantial | First 2 weeks technique/ramp; rebuild over several weeks |
| >1 year | Treat as near-novice | Beginner linear progression; expect faster than first-time progress |
| Older adults | Larger losses | Longer ramp, lighter loads, 3+ RIR |

- Meta-analysis (103 studies): cessation reduces submaximal strength (SMD −0.62), maximal force (−0.46), power (−0.20); losses grow with break length and are larger in >65 y and inactive people. Older-adult muscle size: no significant drop at 12–24 wk, significant at 31–52 wk. (Moderate/Strong) [9][19]
- **Practitioner estimates, not measured results:** "regain in about half the layoff time (range ⅓–⅔)", "younger adults mostly fine up to ~28 days", "older adults lose ~2×". Only the direction (larger losses in >65 y) is from the meta-analysis. [9][11]
- Cap weekly load jumps at ≈5–10% and keep 3–5 RIR in the first two weeks.

## Poor recovery (sleep, stress, deficit)

| State | Volume | Intensity / RIR |
|---|---|---|
| Good sleep (7–9 h), low stress | Planned | Planned |
| 1–2 poor nights | Keep volume; trim last set/accessory if needed | Keep loads; +1 RIR on multi-joint lifts; no PR chasing |
| Chronic poor sleep (<6 h) or high stress >1 wk | Cut ~20–40% of sets | +1–2 RIR; avoid failure; stable lifts you know; consider early deload |
| Large calorie deficit | Lower-middle of the range | Keep load, remove junk volume |

Evidence: 17 studies, moderate/weak quality; one night of total deprivation has limited effect on strength; consecutive nights of restriction reduce force in multi-joint but not single-joint tasks. No hypertrophy-specific review found. Table values are Practitioner. [20]

## Sedentary and obese beginners

- Screen first (e.g. PAR-Q+); sedentary and age ≥45–50, or with risk factors → start at the low end, RPE ≤6 for the first 2–4 weeks. (Practitioner)
- Starting dose: 2 d/wk resistance, 1–2 sets × 10–15 of 6–8 machine/low-impact moves, 3–5 RIR for the first 2–4 weeks; walking daily; aerobic start ~30 min/day and build gradually. (Practitioner; ACSM weight-management stand read via secondary summaries, low reliability) [21]
- Weight loss goal: ~250–300 min/wk moderate activity for greater loss and less regain. [21]
- Avoid high-impact jumping initially; after 4–6 weeks progress ~5–10% per week as tolerated.
- Joint pain on weight-bearing moves → seated/supported variants, cycling or pool-based aerobic.
- ACSM 2026 excludes obesity, sarcopenia and frailty from its scope; no population-specific resistance guidance was found. Defaults here are Practitioner. [1]

## Decision rules

1. IF chest pain, exertional dizziness/syncope, known CV/metabolic/renal disease, uncontrolled hypertension, recent surgery, pregnancy complications or sudden neurological symptoms THEN do not build a hard program; advise clinical clearance.
2. IF user asks for diagnosis/treatment of an injury THEN decline, give general loading principles, refer.
3. IF training history <6–12 months OR returning after >1 year THEN beginner template: full body 2–3 d/wk, 4–6 simple exercises, 8 sets/muscle/wk, 3 RIR, linear progression.
4. IF linear progression stalls ≥2–3 consecutive attempts THEN intermediate: double progression, upper/lower or PPL, ~12 sets/muscle/wk.
5. IF progress is slower than monthly after >2–3 years THEN advanced: periodized blocks, deload every 4–8 weeks.
6. IF level unclear THEN intermediate-lite (~10 sets, 2–3 RIR).
7. IF age ≥65 and healthy THEN 2–3 full-body resistance days, 1–3 sets × 8–12, 3 RIR, 1–2 power sets, balance so multicomponent totals ≥3 d/wk, no routine failure.
8. IF older adult has poor balance or fall history THEN supported variants and refer for fall-risk assessment.
9. IF user is female THEN same rules as male; IF asks for cycle periodization THEN state evidence does not support it and offer symptom-based autoregulation.
10. IF pregnant AND cleared THEN moderate RT + ~150 min/wk aerobic, RPE-guided; IF postpartum THEN only after clearance, low start; leakage/pelvic pain → refer.
11. IF age <18 THEN supervised, technique-first, 1–3 sets × 6–15, 2–3 non-consecutive days.
12. IF 1 day/wk THEN full body, 5–6 compounds, 1–2 hard sets each; expect mostly strength.
13. IF 2–3 days of 30–45 min THEN full body, antagonist supersets, 2 sets each.
14. IF hypertrophy goal AND time short THEN prioritize total weekly hard sets over days.
15. IF only bodyweight THEN hardest variation allowing ≥8 reps, 15–30 reps at 1–3 RIR, leverage first.
16. IF pain sharp, worsening, radiating, night, swelling or post-trauma THEN stop movement and refer; ELSE substitute within pattern and apply the ≤3/10 rule.
17. IF off <2 wk THEN resume; 2–4 wk THEN −10–20% loads for one week; >1 month THEN week 1 ≈ ⅓–½ loads and ramp; >1 year THEN beginner progression.
18. IF sleep <6 h for several nights or stress high THEN cut volume 20–40%, +1–2 RIR, favor stable lifts.
19. IF BMI ≥30 or deconditioned THEN walking + machine/low-impact RT, 1–2 sets, RPE 5–6.

## Common mistakes

- Giving beginners advanced volume or complexity (high-volume splits, Olympic lifts, failure sets).
- Giving older adults only light "toning" with no power, balance or heavy-enough strength work; or pushing them to failure.
- Women's "light weights, high reps" programs or cycle-phase periodization with no evidence base.
- Treating youth as small adults, or banning youth resistance training.
- 6-day plans for time-poor users.
- Believing bodyweight cannot build muscle, or doing high reps far from failure.
- Resuming a layoff at pre-layoff loads, or being too timid and ignoring fast regain.
- Stating "⅓ volume maintains muscle" for older adults (Bickel: strength yes, size no).
- Stating "half the layoff" as a measured regain time.
- Stacking high volume on poor sleep and high stress.
- Pushing through pain, or giving diagnostic/treatment claims instead of substituting and referring.
- Copying ACSM 2026 numbers to clinical populations.

## Evidence notes

- ACSM 2026 (Currier et al.): "2–3 RIR" is a hedged suggestion ("insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets"); "training experience minimal impact" is carried over from the authors' 2023 network meta-analysis and most evidence is from novices; no inter-set rest recommendation; 2009 day-count staging rested on 3 studies (n=59). [1]
- Menstrual cycle: low-quality studies. Absence of evidence is not proof of no effect. [6]
- Power training in older adults: modest, low-certainty. [7]
- Pain thresholds are conventions from rehab research, not validated for general lifting. [18]
- Youth numbers, several older-adult rep ranges, and sedentary/obese resistance dosing are background knowledge or Practitioner, not retrieved sources.
- Sleep effects on hypertrophy (vs strength) have no retrieved high-quality evidence.
- Muscle memory: faster retraining is more certain than the mechanism (myonuclear permanence is contested). [22]
- A minimal-dose SportRxiv-style threshold ("~4 sets/wk for detectable hypertrophy") appeared in a Wave 1 summary and was **not verifiable**; omitted.
- Pregnancy, youth and WHO ≥2 d/wk specifics were read from secondary summaries.
