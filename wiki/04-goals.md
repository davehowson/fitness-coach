# Goals and Training Types

> Purpose: map a stated goal to the adaptations, variables, weekly structure and priorities of a program, and decide how to blend several goals.
> Use when: the user names a goal (or none), wants more than one goal, or you must choose modalities for a goal.
> Depends on: [principles](01-principles.md), [intake](02-intake.md), [variables](03-variables.md), [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md), [conditioning](09-conditioning.md), [hybrid](10-hybrid.md), [populations](11-populations.md)

## Key rules (TL;DR)

1. A goal is a bundle of target adaptations. It sets rep range, load, rest, exercise choice and weekly structure. Pick **one primary goal** and **at most 1–2 secondary goals** per block. (Practitioner)
2. Strength = load-dependent: **≥80% 1RM, 1–6 reps**, 3 min rest. Hypertrophy = load-flexible: any load 30–100% 1RM works if sets are hard; practical **6–15 reps** (up to 30). (Strong for hypertrophy load-independence; Moderate for strength loads) [1][2]
3. Hypertrophy weekly hard sets per muscle: **8 beginner / 12 intermediate / 16 advanced or priority** (ranges 6–10 / 10–20 / 12–20). Max strength: **6** sets per lift-specific muscle (4–10), as 2–3 sets × 2–3 sessions. Frequency default **2×/week per muscle**. Full tables: [variables](03-variables.md).
4. Default effort **2 RIR (RPE 8)**, range 1–3; heavy barbell compounds, beginners, older adults **3 RIR**. Failure is not required. [1]
5. Fat loss is driven by energy balance (diet). Training's job: keep or build lean mass (resistance training is non-negotiable in a deficit), add expenditure (steps, cardio), support adherence. Do not cut loads because calories are low. (Moderate) [8][9]
6. Health floor for any general goal: **150–300 min/wk moderate OR 75–150 min/wk vigorous aerobic + muscle strengthening of all major groups ≥2 d/wk** (1 vigorous min ≈ 2 moderate). (Strong, WHO 2020 / US PAG 2018) [5][6]
7. Compatible pairs: strength+hypertrophy, strength+power, hypertrophy+general health, hypertrophy+recomp. Competing pairs: power or max strength vs high-volume endurance. [10][11]
8. Rule of thumb for blends: primary gets ~60–70% of weekly hard-set volume, secondary gets maintenance (4–6 sets/muscle/wk) to moderate. **Unsourced heuristic (Practitioner), not evidence.**
9. Modality matters less than a goal-matched stimulus. Machines, free weights, bodyweight and kettlebells all work; test-specificity is the main differentiator. Olympic lifts and plyometrics are power tools with higher skill and injury demand. (Moderate) [12][14][15]
10. No goal given → default to general health: 3 full-body days, walking/easy aerobic to the floor in rule 6, 10 min mobility.
11. Mortality data (muscle strengthening, cardiorespiratory fitness) are observational. Cite as association, never as causal proof. [7][8b]

## Goal → adaptation map

| Adaptation family | Dominant in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Neural (recruitment, rate coding, coordination) | Max strength, power; early gains in everything | Specific to load, velocity, pattern |
| Muscular (fibre cross-sectional area) | Hypertrophy | Weekly hard sets + effort drive it |
| Metabolic / cardiovascular (mitochondria, stroke volume, capillaries) | Endurance | See [conditioning](09-conditioning.md) |
| Connective / range of motion | Mobility, power (tendon stiffness) | Tolerance to end-range tension |

Specificity: adaptations are specific to the load, velocity, contraction type, pattern and energy system trained. Match variables to the goal. [1]

Repetition-continuum heuristic (1–5 reps strength, 8–12 hypertrophy, 15+ endurance) is directionally right for strength and endurance but **too narrow for hypertrophy**. (Moderate) [2]

### Per-goal programming notes

**1. Max strength.** Definition: peak force (1RM). Adaptations: neural drive, some hypertrophy, tendon stiffness. Looks like: main barbell lift(s) first, low reps, long rests, few accessories. 2–3 sets per exercise (ACSM 2026) [1]. Prioritize heavy compounds, technique, progressive load, frequent practice of the target lift. De-emphasize high-rep burn sets, high cardio volume, failure on heavy lifts. If only light loads are available, expect slower strength gains; hypertrophy is unaffected. [2]

**2. Hypertrophy.** Definition: muscle size. Looks like: compounds plus isolations, each muscle 2×/wk, progress reps then load. Prioritize weekly hard sets per muscle, effort near failure, progression. De-emphasize max-single testing and excess cardio. If heavy loads are not tolerated (injury, preference), use ≥15 reps near failure; hypertrophy is similar. Diminishing returns ~18–20 weekly sets; do not exceed ~22 even for short specialization. [1][3]

**3. Power.** Definition: rate of force development (force × velocity). Looks like: first in session, fully rested, low reps, jumps, throws, kettlebell swings, Olympic derivatives (~60–85% 1RM). Prioritize speed intent and quality. De-emphasize fatigue and grinding sets. Plyometrics: sprint gains were associated with ≥15 sessions over <10 weeks; vertical jump with >10 weeks and >20 sessions (Moderate); do not use plyometrics as "cardio". Beginners: jumps, throws and swings before full Olympic lifts; teach landing first. Older adults: fast concentric at 40–70% 1RM. [13][14][15][19]

**4. Muscular endurance.** Definition: repeated submaximal efforts. Looks like: 15–30+ reps, circuits, 60–90 s rest. Prioritize rep volume at light loads. De-emphasize max-load work. (Practitioner) [4]

**5. Cardio endurance.** Aerobic capacity and economy. Prioritize weekly aerobic dose and progressive duration; mostly easy plus 1–2 hard sessions/wk. De-emphasize heavy leg volume near key sessions. Details in [conditioning](09-conditioning.md).

**6. General fitness / health.** Broad capacity: aerobic, strength, mobility, balance. Prioritize consistency and full-body coverage. De-emphasize specialization and extreme volume. Time-limited (≤3 h/wk): 2 full-body resistance sessions + brisk walking or short intervals to reach the aerobic floor. [5][6]

**7. Fat loss / recomposition.** Reduce fat mass, keep or build lean mass. Looks like: resistance training 3–4×/wk as the base, daily steps, moderate cardio only as needed for expenditure; diet creates the deficit. Exercise alone at <250 min/wk gives modest loss; >250 min/wk is associated with clinically significant loss and less regain (Strong) [8]. Resistance training protects lean mass; aerobic alone does not (Moderate) [9]. Lean lifters in a deficit: slower loss, avoid deep deficits; ISSN suggests protein up to 2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass for lean resistance-trained dieters [9]. The common 1.6–2.2 g/kg figure is practice, not a retrieved source (Practitioner). Recomposition is plausible in beginners, returners and people with higher body fat; evidence is thin for advanced lean trainees (Practitioner). Prioritize lean-mass retention, protein, adherence. De-emphasize cardio-only plans, "toning" high-rep-only plans, crash diets.

**8. Longevity.** Deliver both aerobic floor and strength floor. Muscle-strengthening cohorts show 10–17% lower risk of all-cause mortality, CVD, cancer and diabetes, apparent peak benefit at ~30–60 min/wk (J-shaped); combined with aerobic activity all-cause mortality risk was ~40% lower vs none (Moderate-to-low, observational) [7]. Higher cardiorespiratory fitness tracks with lower mortality (elite vs low fitness ~80% lower; referred clinical sample, observational) [8b]. Add power and balance for ≥65. See [populations](11-populations.md).

**9. Mobility / flexibility.** Strength training through full ROM and stretching produced similar ROM gains in 11 RCTs; static or PNF beat ballistic for long-term ROM; volume, intensity and frequency were not significant moderators (Moderate) [16]. ACSM 2011: stretch to tightness, accumulate ~60 s per stretch (2–4 reps of 15–30 s), warm muscles; 2–3 d/wk minimum frequency is unconfirmed, daily is fine [17]. Do not make long static stretching the whole warm-up before max-effort power work (see [session design](05-session-design.md)).

**10. Athletic / sport.** Do a needs analysis (energy system, movement pattern, injury profile), build a strength base, then power, then sport-specific; adjust by season. In-season, drop generic bodybuilding volume. (Practitioner)

**11. Functional fitness.** Two meanings. (a) Daily-task capacity (balance, sit-to-stand, carrying), emphasized for older adults: multi-joint patterns, balance, power. (b) CrossFit-style high-intensity functional training: injury prevalence ≈35% ± 15%, ≈3 injuries/1,000 h, comparable to other exercise, low-quality studies; barbell lifts under fatigue (deadlift, snatch, clean & jerk, press) are the main sources (Practitioner/Moderate) [18]. Beginners wanting this style: scale loads, use machines, kettlebells or dumbbells, avoid snatch/clean at high fatigue and high reps.

**12. Hybrid (strength + endurance).** 2–3 lifting days + 2–4 aerobic days; one quality per session; hard sessions separated. Full rules: [hybrid](10-hybrid.md).

## Goal-compatibility matrix

| Pair | Compatibility | Why / constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Strength + hypertrophy | High | Same toolset; strength = heavier, hypertrophy = more volume |
| Strength + power | High | Power first in session; strength supports power |
| Hypertrophy + fat loss/recomp | High (beginners, returners, higher body fat) | Resistance training is the base |
| Strength/hypertrophy + general health | High | Add the aerobic floor |
| Strength/hypertrophy + cardio endurance | Medium | Interference for max strength (SMD −0.06) and hypertrophy (−0.01) is negligible; only same-session/<3 h gap hurts, and only explosive strength (−0.28). Huiberts: male lower-body strength −0.43, female data sparse [10][11] |
| Hypertrophy + high-volume endurance | Medium–Low | Fatigue and energy cost; keep endurance to 2–3 sessions/wk |
| Power + high-volume endurance | Low | Explosive strength is the quality that suffers; keep aerobic volume low, separate from power sessions [10] |
| Max strength + large deficit | Medium–Low | Aim to maintain strength; lean-mass gains unlikely in lean athletes [9] |
| Mobility + any | High | Add 10–15 min |

Grade: Moderate for the interference numbers (Schumann 2022); the rest of the matrix is Practitioner synthesis.

## Blending rules

1. One primary goal; at most 1–2 secondary. Do not mix max strength, hypertrophy, marathon base and power in one block.
2. **Unsourced heuristic (Practitioner):** primary goal gets ~60–70% of weekly hard-set volume. No trial tests blend ratios. The qualitative support is that a moderate endurance dose does not cost strength or size [10][11].
3. Secondary goal dose: maintenance (resistance: **4–6 sets/muscle/wk**, 1–2 sessions/wk, loads kept heavy) up to moderate.

| Scenario | Primary share | Secondary share | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong primary, light secondary | ~70% of weekly hard sessions | Maintenance | Strength with 2 easy aerobic sessions |
| Equal hybrid | ~50/50 | ~50 | 2 lift days + 2 run days + 1 mixed |
| Health-first | Resistance 2–3 d + aerobic floor | Mobility/balance 10–15 min | General fitness |

(Practitioner; no direct trial.)

4. Lifting primary + aerobic secondary: 2–3 aerobic sessions/wk, prefer cycling/rowing/incline walking, keep hard intervals away from heavy leg days; same session → lift first if strength is the priority; separate hard cardio and heavy legs by ≥6 h (≥24 h before key quality sessions). [10][11]
5. ≤3 days/wk with multiple goals: full-body sessions covering both (heavy compound + one conditioning finisher). Pick one primary anyway.

## Training modalities overview

| Modality | Best for | Strengths | Limits | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free weights | Strength, hypertrophy, power | Transfer to free-weight tests; scalable | Technique and stabilization demands | Moderate [12] |
| Machines | Hypertrophy, beginners, isolation | Easy to learn; safe near failure | Less transfer to free-weight tests | Moderate [12] |
| Bodyweight / calisthenics | General fitness, endurance, travel, home | No equipment | Leg loading hard to progress; needs variation ladders | Practitioner |
| Kettlebells | Power endurance, conditioning, grip | Ballistic hinge, compact | Strength and aerobic evidence equivocal | Practitioner [18] |
| Plyometrics | Power, jump, sprint | Little equipment | Tissue stress; landing skill | Moderate [14] |
| Olympic lifts / derivatives | Power, sport | High force × velocity; CMJ ES 0.41, sprint ES −0.44, squat 1RM ES 0.81 | High skill; injury risk in fatigue; superiority over plyometrics or traditional lifts not established | Moderate [15] |
| CrossFit-style metcons | GPP, variety | Community, density | Technical lifts under fatigue; poor progression control | Practitioner [18] |
| Circuits | Time efficiency, endurance, fat-loss support | Density | Lower per-lift load, less strength | Practitioner |

Free-weight vs machine head-to-head (13 studies, 1,016 participants): no significant difference in dynamic strength, isometric strength or jump gains; larger gains on the tested modality. [12]

## Decision rules

1. IF goal = max strength THEN main lifts 1–6 reps at ≥80% 1RM, 3 min rest, each target lift 2–3×/wk, 2–3 sets per exercise, accessories 6–12 reps.
2. IF goal = hypertrophy THEN weekly sets 8 / 12 / 16 by level, 2 sessions/muscle/wk, compounds 6–15 reps, isolation 10–30, 2 RIR; progress reps then load.
3. IF heavy loads are not tolerated AND goal = hypertrophy THEN ≥15 reps near failure.
4. IF goal = power THEN power work first, fully rested, 1–5 reps (3–6 contacts), stop when speed drops, 2 sessions/wk for ≥8–10 weeks; beginners use jumps/throws/swings before Olympic lifts.
5. IF goal = muscular endurance THEN 15–30+ reps at ~40–60% 1RM or bodyweight, 60–90 s rest.
6. IF goal = general health AND no experience THEN 2–3 full-body sessions (1–3 sets × 8–15, all major groups) + aerobic floor; add balance if age ≥65.
7. IF goal = fat loss THEN resistance 3–4×/wk at moderate-hard effort, steps first, cardio only as needed; diet owns the deficit; keep loads.
8. IF dieting AND lean AND wants to keep muscle THEN slower loss, avoid deep deficits, maintain load.
9. IF primary = strength/hypertrophy AND secondary = aerobic THEN 2–3 sessions/wk, low-impact preferred, separated from heavy legs.
10. IF primary = power/sprint AND adds endurance THEN keep aerobic volume low and separate from power sessions.
11. IF hybrid run + lift THEN one quality per day (heavy lift / hard run / easy run), ≥1 true rest day, hard run and heavy lower-body on different days.
12. IF goal = mobility THEN static or PNF holds 15–60 s, ~60 s total per stretch, warm muscles, plus strength through full ROM.
13. IF goal = longevity THEN both the aerobic floor and ≥2 strength days (~30–60 min/wk of strength); power and balance for ≥65.
14. IF goal = sport THEN needs analysis, strength base → power → sport-specific.
15. IF multiple goals AND ≤3 days/wk THEN full-body sessions, one primary.
16. IF no goal given THEN general-health default (rule 10 above).
17. IF user wants CrossFit-style/circuits AND is a beginner THEN scale loads, no technical barbell lifts under fatigue.
18. IF user prefers machines THEN fine for hypertrophy and machine-task strength; add free weights if free-weight performance is the goal.

## Common mistakes

- Same prescription (e.g., 3×10) for every goal.
- "Toning" or high-rep-only plans for fat loss; "heavy lifting makes women bulky" myth. Load range for hypertrophy is wide. [2]
- Cardio-only fat-loss plans with no resistance training. [9]
- Overestimating exercise vs diet for fat loss.
- Too many simultaneous goals in one block.
- Long cardio before heavy lifts; heavy legs the day before a key long run.
- Olympic lifts or high-rep technical barbell lifts for novices under fatigue. [18]
- Plyometrics as junk-contact cardio.
- No power or balance work for older adults.
- Cutting training load in a deficit instead of keeping intensity.
- Dropping the 2 d/wk strength floor in cardio-focused plans.
- Presenting mortality associations as causal.

## Evidence notes

- **Interference:** Schumann 2022 (43 studies): max strength −0.06, hypertrophy −0.01, explosive −0.28; the only significant moderator is same-session vs ≥3 h gap, and only for explosive strength; training status did not moderate. Wilson 2012 found negative correlations of gains with endurance frequency and duration and interference with running but not cycling; its specific duration/frequency thresholds are unverified and not used here. Running-vs-cycling is Contested. Huiberts: male lower-body strength −0.43, female 0.08 (sparse data). (Moderate/Contested) [10][11]
- **Weekly sets:** ACSM 2026 ≥10 sets/wk for hypertrophy; diminishing returns ~18–20 come from Pelland et al. (peer-reviewed, *Sports Med* 2025/26), so the two are not independent confirmation. Experience-specific set counts (8 / 12 / 16) are Practitioner. [1][3]
- **ACSM 2026** (Currier et al.) is an overview of 137 reviews in healthy adults; it excludes obesity, sarcopenia and frailty. It gives no inter-set rest recommendation (rest values here are canonical practitioner/meta-analytic defaults, see [variables](03-variables.md)). 2–3 RIR is the authors' suggestion; they state there is insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets. [1]
- **Blend ratio 60–70%:** no source. Practitioner.
- **Resistance vs cardio for fat loss:** differences vanish when energy expenditure is matched; Lafontant 2025 meta-analysis results were not read. HIIT vs MICT fat loss is small and inconsistent (see [conditioning](09-conditioning.md)).
- **Power training in older adults:** low-certainty modest edge over traditional strength training for function. [19]
- **Kettlebells:** no pooled meta-analysis in healthy adults found. **CrossFit injury rates:** low-quality studies.
- **ACSM 2011 flexibility frequency:** exact text not confirmed.
- **Mortality associations:** observational; Momma full-text GRADE for site-specific cancer outcomes is "very low".
- US PAG third edition is in development; check for a newer edition if the date matters. [20]
