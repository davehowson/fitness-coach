# Resistance-Training Variables

> Purpose: set every resistance-training dial (sets, load, effort, frequency, reps, rest, tempo, ROM, selection, order, techniques) for a given goal.
> Use when: choosing numbers for any exercise, session or week; checking a draft plan against defaults.
> Depends on: [principles](01-principles.md), [goals](04-goals.md); consumed by [session design](05-session-design.md), [splits](06-splits.md), [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md), [progression](08-progression.md).

## Key rules (TL;DR)

1. **Weekly hard sets per muscle is the master dial.** Hypertrophy defaults: beginner **8**, intermediate **12**, advanced/priority muscle **16** (ranges 6–10 / 10–20 / 12–20). Diminishing returns ~18–20; strength flattens much sooner than hypertrophy. [1][2] Moderate
2. **Count sets fractionally:** direct set = 1, indirect (synergist) set = 0.5. Count only hard working sets, never warm-up sets. [2] Moderate
3. **Per-session soft cap: 10 fractional hard sets per muscle (≈6–8 direct).** If the weekly target needs more, add a session. ~11 fractional (hypertrophy) and ~2 direct (strength) are "points of undetectable further benefit" from a preprint, not ceilings. [3] Contested
4. **Effort: 2 RIR (RPE 8)**, range 1–3. Last set of an isolation exercise may go 0–1 RIR. Beginners, older adults and heavy barbell compounds: **3 RIR**. Failure is not required. [1][7][8] Moderate
5. **Load:** hypertrophy works at any load 30–100% 1RM if sets are hard (practical 6–15 reps, up to 30); strength needs **≥80% 1RM, 1–6 reps**; power 30–70% 1RM with maximal-velocity intent. [1][9][10] Strong (hypertrophy), Moderate (strength, power)
6. **Frequency: 2×/muscle/week.** 1× is fine below ~10 sets/wk; 3× for high volume. At equal weekly volume, frequency barely affects hypertrophy; strength benefits from ≥2 sessions/week. [1][5][6] Strong (hypertrophy), Moderate (strength)
7. **Rest long enough to repeat target reps. Never default below 60 s.** Heavy compound (≥80% / ≤6 reps) **3 min**; hypertrophy compound **2 min**; isolation/machine **90 s**. [11][12][13] Moderate
8. **Tempo controlled, 0.5–8 s per rep; avoid reps >10 s. ROM full by default;** lengthened partials are an acceptable option. [14][15] Moderate / Contested
9. **Order: priority, most technical, most fatiguing first:** power → strength → hypertrophy → isolation → core → conditioning. [16] Strong (strength), Moderate (hypertrophy)
10. **Advanced techniques (supersets, drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps) save time; they are not better per set.** Default to straight sets unless time is short. Antagonist supersets cut time with no outcome loss. [19][20] Moderate

## Core concepts

- **Stimulus comes from hard sets**: sets near failure with enough load to recruit high-threshold motor units. Every variable below changes how many hard sets are done, how heavy, how often, how recoverable.
- **Volume = hard working sets per muscle per week**, not tonnage and not "exercises". Direct set: muscle is the prime mover. Indirect set: muscle is a synergist (bench → triceps, front delts).
- **Intensity has three languages:** %1RM (load), RIR (reps in reserve), RPE (RPE 10 = failure, RPE 9 ≈ 1 RIR, RPE 8 ≈ 2 RIR). RIR estimates are poor beyond ~4–5 reps from failure and beginners underestimate reps left. [8]
- **Load and proximity to failure are partly independent:** light load near failure and heavy load with 3 RIR are both hard; light load far from failure is not.
- **Old "repetition continuum"** (1–5 strength, 6–12 hypertrophy, 15+ endurance) is a rough default, not a law: hypertrophy is load-agnostic when sets are hard; strength is load-specific. [9]

## 1. Volume

### 1.1 Set counting (fractional)

Weekly muscle volume = Σ direct sets + 0.5 × Σ indirect sets. Indirect-only sets count 0.5, not 0. Fractional counting fit hypertrophy data better than direct-only counting (indirect = 0). [2] Moderate

| Exercise | Direct (1.0) | Indirect (0.5) |
|---|---|---|
| Bench press | Chest | Triceps, front delts |
| Overhead press | Front/side delts | Triceps, upper chest |
| Row / pulldown | Lats, mid-back | Biceps, rear delts |
| Squat | Quads | Glutes, adductors |
| RDL / deadlift | Hamstrings, glutes (RDL), erectors | Quads (conventional), traps/lats (minor) |
| Curl | Biceps | Forearms |

Muscle-to-exercise mapping is Practitioner. Do not credit the same triceps work twice (pressing + extensions both count; do the sum).

### 1.2 Weekly hard sets per muscle

| Goal / population | Default | Range | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy, beginner | **8** | 6–10 | Practitioner |
| Hypertrophy, intermediate | **12** | 10–20; diminishing returns ~18–20 | Moderate [1][2] |
| Hypertrophy, advanced / priority muscle | **16** | 12–20 (≤~22 short-term specialization) | Practitioner |
| Maximal strength (lift-specific muscles) | **6** (2–3 sets × 2–3 sessions) | 4–10 | Moderate [1][2] |
| Maintenance | **4–6**, heavy loads, 1–2 sessions/wk | 3–6 | Moderate [23][24] |
| Minimum useful (general fitness) | ≥1 hard set per major pattern, ≥2 days/wk | — | Moderate [27][29] |
| Muscular endurance | 6–12 | — | Practitioner |
| Power (specific pattern) | 3–9 power sets | ACSM: ≤~24 total reps per exercise per session | Moderate [1] |

Notes:
- ACSM's "~18–20 sets" diminishing-returns figure comes *from* Pelland, so it is not independent confirmation. [1][2]
- Experience staging by speed of progress, not years: beginner = progresses session to session; intermediate = week to week; advanced = month to month.
- Volume landmarks (MV/MEV/MAV/MRV, Renaissance Periodization) are heuristics from a third-party summary with no peer-reviewed validation. Use only as rough anchors (MV ≈ 4–6, MEV ≈ 6–10, MAV ≈ 10–20, MRV ≈ 16–25, individual). Practitioner [25]
- Not verified, do not state as fact: "~1 set/wk minimum for strength", "~4 sets/wk minimum for hypertrophy".

### 1.3 Per-session cap and sets per exercise

- **Soft cap 10 fractional hard sets per muscle per session (≈6–8 direct).** Above it, move volume to another session. Contested [3]
- The cap is a practical choice under the one quantified point (~11 fractional, preprint). The ~6–10 "direct sets per session" figure has no primary source. A newer unreviewed preprint (Boutagy & McKean, 2026) suggests weekly volume, not its split across sessions, drives hypertrophy, which would weaken per-session caps. Treat as unreviewed.
- **Sets per exercise: 2–4** (strength 2–3 per ACSM 2026); up to 5 for peaking or technique blocks. Moderate [1]

## 2. Intensity: load, RIR, RPE

### 2.1 Effort (RIR)

| Situation | Target |
|---|---|
| Default working set | **2 RIR (RPE 8)**; range 1–3 |
| Isolation, last set | 0–1 RIR allowed |
| Beginners, older adults, heavy barbell compounds | **3 RIR (RPE 7)** |
| Strength main lifts | 1–3 RIR; peak singles/doubles RPE 9–10 only occasionally |
| Power | Far from failure (3+ RIR or ≤10–20% velocity/height loss) |

Basis: ACSM 2026 says failure is not necessary and suggests "near-failure or 2–3 RIR", but also states there is insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets. [1] Refalo 2023/2024: no advantage of failure over RIR for hypertrophy. [7] Robinson 2024: hypertrophy improves slightly as sets end closer to failure (exploratory, RIR estimated); strength insensitive to RIR. [8] 2 RIR is the compromise. Grade: Moderate for "2–3 RIR adequate, failure not required"; experience-specific RIR values are Practitioner.

IF user cannot judge RIR THEN prescribe rep targets with a "2-for-2" rule: if the last set beats the top of the rep range by 2 reps, add load next session. Practitioner

### 2.2 Load

| Goal | Load | Reps | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max strength | **≥80% 1RM** (peak sets 85–95%) | **1–6** (1–5 on main lifts) | Moderate [1][9][10] |
| Hypertrophy | Any 30–100% 1RM if sets are hard; default 60–80% | **6–15** practical (up to 30); compounds 6–15, isolations 10–20 | Strong [9][10] |
| Power | 30–70% 1RM, max-velocity intent; 75–90% for heavy-power/Olympic lifts | 1–5 per set | Moderate [1] |
| Muscular endurance | ≤60–67% 1RM | 15–30+ | Practitioner |
| General fitness | Moderate | 8–15 | Practitioner |

Evidence: low- vs high-load to failure gives similar hypertrophy; high load (>60% 1RM) gives greater 1RM. [9] Network meta-analysis: hypertrophy no load difference; strength high and moderate > low. [10] ACSM 2026 found no hypertrophy effect of load across 30–100% 1RM; it did not *recommend* that range.

IF equipment caps load (e.g. dumbbells ≤25 kg) THEN use 20–30 reps at 0–2 RIR; low loads work if sets are near failure.
IF joint pain under heavy load THEN use 12–25 reps at 1–2 RIR; hypertrophy stays similar.

## 3. Frequency

- **Default 2×/muscle/week.** Hypertrophy: 1 vs >5 days/wk no difference when weekly volume is equated. [1][5] Strong
- Strength: ≥2 sessions/wk (upper limit undeterminable); practise competition lifts 2–4×/wk with low per-session sets. [1][6] Moderate. In Grgic 2018 the volume-equated subgroup showed no frequency effect, so frequency is mostly a volume-distribution tool.
- Higher frequency makes high weekly volume easier to sustain under the per-session cap. Use extra days to cap per-session sets, not as a goal.
- IF ≤2 training days/week THEN full-body each session; still hit ≥4–6 sets/muscle/wk.
- IF only 1 session per muscle per week is possible THEN keep it; allow 8–12 sets in that session but ≤~10 fractional; expect slightly less efficient hypertrophy.
- Legacy ACSM 2009 day-count staging (novice 2–3, intermediate 3–4, advanced 4–5 d/wk) rested on 3 studies (n=59). Practitioner only. [22]
- Mapping frequency to a week: see [splits](06-splits.md), [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md).

## 4. Rep ranges

| Exercise type | Reps |
|---|---|
| Heavy barbell compound (squat, deadlift, bench, press, row) | 5–10 (strength end 3–6) |
| Machine, cable, isolation | 10–20 (up to 30 for calves/delts/arms if joint-friendly) |
| Strength main lift | 1–6 |
| Endurance | 15–30+ |
| Power | 1–5 |

## 5. Rest between sets

Rule: rest long enough to repeat target reps on the next set.

| Situation | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy compound (≥80% 1RM or ≤6 reps), strength | **3 min** | 2–5 min (≥3 for trained) |
| Compound, hypertrophy (6–15 reps) | **2 min** | 90 s–3 min |
| Isolation / machine | **90 s** | 60–120 s |
| Beginner | 90–120 s | 60–120 s |
| Time-poor | 60–90 s, or antagonist supersets | never <60 s on compounds |
| Power | 2–5 min (full recovery) | — |
| Endurance | 60–90 s (30–60 s only in light-load endurance circuits; Practitioner) | — |

Basis: >60 s beats ≤60 s slightly for hypertrophy; no appreciable benefit >90 s [11]. Single RCT, trained men: 3 min > 1 min for quad thickness and squat/bench 1RM [12]. Strength: >2 min needed to maximise in trained; 60–120 s adequate in untrained [13]. 2–3 min give similar rep performance [28]. **ACSM 2026 gives no inter-set rest recommendation**; the "3–5 min for heavy loads" figure is from ACSM 2009 [22]. Grade: Moderate (≥90 s hypertrophy, ≥2 min strength), Practitioner (compound/isolation split).

IF reps drop >30% from set to set due to short rest THEN lengthen rest or cut sets or load. Volume load is the driver.

## 6. Tempo

- **Controlled, 0.5–8 s per rep; avoid reps >10 s.** Similar hypertrophy across 0.5–8 s; >10 s inferior (few studies). [14] Moderate
- Practical default: ~1–2 s concentric, 2–3 s controlled eccentric, no pause. Strength: controlled eccentric (~1–3 s), maximal concentric intent. Power: concentric as fast as possible.
- ACSM 2026: eccentric emphasis outperformed concentric-only; no phase-by-phase tempo is firmly established. [1]
- Do not prescribe arbitrary time-under-tension targets.

## 7. Range of motion

- **Full ROM by default.** Overall trivial edge for full ROM in hypertrophy; partials at shortened lengths inferior; partials at lengthened positions equal or possibly superior (reported SMD ≈ 0.28, not verified). [15] Moderate / Contested
- Strength and competition lifts: use competition ROM and pause style. ACSM 2026 favours full ROM for strength.
- IF exercise loads a stretched muscle (RDL, incline curl, overhead triceps extension, deep squat, Bulgarian split squat, cable fly) THEN full ROM with controlled stretch; optionally finish a set with 3–8 lengthened partials.
- IF a joint limits the bottom range THEN use the full range you can control; no bouncing.

## 8. Exercise selection principles

1. Cover weekly: squat/knee-dominant, hinge, horizontal push, vertical push, horizontal pull, vertical pull, lunge/single-leg; plus calves, core, arms as needed. [1] Practitioner
2. Compound vs isolation: similar whole-muscle hypertrophy; isolation can add region-specific work (hypothesis). [17] Moderate
3. Free weights vs machines: similar strength and hypertrophy when volume and effort match; free weights favoured by specificity for a free-weight test lift. Beginners or low skill/time: stable machines and simple compounds are fine. [1][26] Moderate
4. Volume needed but systemic fatigue high → add machine/isolation sets, not more squats/deadlifts.
5. Add 1 isolation exercise at long muscle length where regions matter (overhead extension for triceps long head, leg extension for rectus femoris, incline press for upper chest). [17] Hypothesis
6. Variation: keep main lifts constant and progress them; rotate accessories every ~4–8 weeks or when stale/painful. Varying exercises every session gave the same results with higher motivation; random per-session variation is not needed and hurts overload tracking. [18] Moderate (single small RCT)
7. Unilateral vs bilateral: equal hypertrophy; strength follows specificity (bilateral lifts for bilateral strength). See [session design](05-session-design.md).
8. IF pain/injury on an exercise THEN substitute a similar-pattern machine or dumbbell option; keep weekly sets constant.
9. Concrete exercises per pattern: [exercise library](12-exercise-library.md).

## 9. Exercise order

- **Priority / most technical / most fatiguing first:** power → strength → hypertrophy → isolation → core → conditioning. Larger before smaller muscle groups, multi-joint before single-joint. [16][22]
- Exercises placed first gain more strength; hypertrophy about equal regardless of order (g≈0.03). [16] Strong (strength), Moderate (hypertrophy)
- IF a muscle is lagging THEN place its exercise first; pre-exhaust is unnecessary.
- IF two lifts are equally important THEN alternate which goes first week to week.
- IF antagonist superset THEN order within pair is minor; the heavy lift leads.
- IF conditioning is in the session THEN after lifting unless endurance is the day's priority ([conditioning](09-conditioning.md), [hybrid](10-hybrid.md)).

## 10. Advanced techniques

| Technique | Evidence | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antagonist superset | Similar hypertrophy and strength; sessions ~36–50% shorter; agonist-antagonist pairs maintain reps (SMD +0.68); same-muscle pairs cut volume load (SMD −1.08) [20] Moderate | Default time-saver |
| Drop set | Trivial effect favouring drop sets (ES 0.08 hypertrophy, 0.07 strength) but ~½–⅓ the time at similar volume [19] Moderate | Last isolation set: 1–2 sets/exercise, 2–3 drops, −20–30% load |
| Rest-pause / myo-reps | Low-quality direct evidence for rest-pause; none controlled for myo-reps Practitioner | Activation set to ~1–3 RIR, then 3–5 mini-sets of ~3–5 reps with 10–20 s rest; isolation/machine only |
| Cluster sets | No strength (ES −0.05) or hypertrophy (ES −0.05) advantage; reduce fatigue [21] Moderate | Power/velocity work only |

Rules:
- IF time is not the constraint THEN straight sets.
- IF using drop sets, rest-pause or myo-reps THEN isolation/machine only, ≤1–2 per muscle per session, count the extra reps as ~1–2 additional effective sets, avoid for beginners and heavy barbell compounds.
- IF a technique raises soreness enough to cut next-session performance THEN remove it.

## Decision rules

- IF goal = hypertrophy AND beginner THEN start 6–10 sets/muscle/wk; add 1–2 sets/wk only when progress stalls and recovery is good.
- IF goal = hypertrophy AND intermediate/advanced THEN 10–20 sets/muscle/wk; start near 10–12 and add ~1 set/muscle/week across a 4–8 week block, then deload ([progression](08-progression.md)).
- IF a muscle is a priority THEN raise toward 16–20 (≤~22 short-term) and hold others at ~6–10.
- IF goal = strength THEN 2–3 sets per exercise per session, 4–10 sets/muscle/wk; spend effort on specific heavy practice, not more sets.
- IF goal = maintenance THEN 4–6 sets/muscle/wk, 1–2 sessions, keep loads heavy; older adults 2 sessions and 2–3 sets; Bickel: ⅓ dose kept older-adult strength but not muscle size. [23][24]
- IF a session would exceed 10 fractional sets for a muscle THEN split across sessions.
- IF performance falls ≥2 sessions, joints ache, or sleep/appetite worsen THEN cut volume 30–50% for a week before changing anything else.
- IF beginner THEN 3 RIR, 90–120 s rest, simple compounds/machines, linear load progression.
- IF time-poor THEN antagonist supersets and shorter isolation rest; keep compound rest ≥90 s; keep sets at ≥2 hard sets per exercise.
- IF user is older adult THEN 3 RIR, avoid routine failure; add power (fast concentric, 40–70% 1RM). See [populations](11-populations.md).

## Common mistakes

- Prescribing 25–30+ sets/muscle/week to beginners, or <6 sets/wk for hypertrophy.
- Counting exercises or warm-up sets as hard sets; ignoring indirect volume (bench + OHP + dips and then 12 more triceps sets).
- Every set to failure, or "RPE 5" sets never near failure.
- Insisting hypertrophy needs 8–12 reps only or that light weights do not count.
- 30–45 s rests on compounds, collapsing volume load.
- Super-slow tempos (≥10 s reps) or time-under-tension targets.
- >10 fractional sets for one muscle in one session instead of spreading.
- Randomising exercises every session; or never changing a painful exercise.
- Isolation before the main compound for a strength goal.
- Treating RP landmarks as laws.
- Drop sets/rest-pause on every set (junk fatigue).
- Citing "3–5 min rest" as ACSM 2026, or the per-session numbers as ceilings.

## Evidence notes

- **Volume:** Pelland meta-regression (67 studies, 2,058 participants) is peer-reviewed (*Sports Med* 56(2):481–505, 2025/26); more sets → more hypertrophy and strength with diminishing returns, steeper for strength; fractional counting fit best; effect-size tables not read. [2] Schoenfeld 2017: +0.37% per added weekly set; ≥10 sets > <10 (3.9%); the <5/5–9/10+ split was only a trend. Older, superseded for curve shape. [4] Moderate
- **Per-session volume:** Remmert/Pelland is a **preprint**; ~2 direct (strength) and ~11 fractional (hypertrophy) are "points of undetectable outcome superiority", authors urge caution at high volumes. [3] Contested
- **ACSM 2026** (137 systematic reviews, healthy adults): ≥10 sets/wk hypertrophy, 2–3 sets per exercise for strength, ≥2 sessions/wk, ≥80% 1RM for strength, 30–70% for power, failure unnecessary. Its "experience had minimal impact" line restates the authors' 2023 network meta-analysis, mostly in novices. It excludes obesity, sarcopenia and frailty. [1]
- **Proximity to failure:** Refalo 2023 (ES 0.19 set-failure vs not; 0.12 momentary failure, n.s.) vs Robinson 2024 (closer is slightly better, exploratory). Reconciled as: small benefit near last ~1–3 RIR, failure itself unnecessary. [7][8]
- **Frequency:** Schoenfeld 2019 (25 studies): no difference volume-equated. Grgic 2018: strength gain with frequency vanishes volume-equated. [5][6]
- **Rest:** small meta-analytic benefit >60 s; single-RCT support for 3 min; ACSM 2026 silent. Partly Contested. [11][12][13]
- **ROM, tempo, lengthened partials:** short trials, mostly lower-body or single-site outcomes. [14][15]
- **Superset time saving figures (36–50%)** come from the 2025 meta-analysis; drop-set time saving from Coleman. [19][20] Rest-pause/myo-rep protocols: Practitioner convention.
- **Unverified / do not state as fact:** minimum-set thresholds attributed to Pelland full text; RP landmark validity; menstrual-cycle programming (not supported).
