# Training Principles & Program Design Process

> Purpose: decide what a good plan is, which principles every plan must encode, and in what order to design one.
> Use when: starting any plan, or judging whether a plan is sound before looking at details.
> Depends on: [intake](02-intake.md) (inputs), [variables](03-variables.md) (numbers), [quality checklist](14-quality-checklist.md) (final gate)

## Key rules (TL;DR)

1. A good plan is a **fit between plan and person**: it serves the goal, delivers enough stimulus, stays inside recovery capacity, fits real constraints (days, minutes, equipment, injuries), and is something the person will actually do. [1][2][3]
2. **Adherence beats optimality.** A good plan that is done beats a perfect plan that is skipped. When time is short, cut sets, not sessions. (Moderate) [9]
3. **Health floor first.** Every plan meets or builds toward: 150–300 min/wk moderate OR 75–150 min/wk vigorous aerobic (1 vigorous min ≈ 2 moderate) AND muscle strengthening of all major groups ≥2 d/wk. Older adults add multicomponent (balance + strength) ≥3 d/wk (WHO 2020; US PAG gives no day count). (Strong) [4][5]
4. **Specificity:** pick reps, load, rest, exercises and conditioning type from the goal. One primary goal, at most 1–2 secondary per block. (Strong) [2][3]
5. **Overload must be written down.** Every main lift gets an explicit progression rule (default: double progression; see [progression](08-progression.md)). "3×10" with no rule is not a plan. (Strong) [1][3]
6. **Volume is the main dose dial.** Default weekly hard sets per muscle: beginner 8, intermediate 12, advanced/priority 16 (hypertrophy); 6 for max strength; 4–6 for maintenance. Diminishing returns ~18–20. (Moderate) [6][7]
7. **Frequency default 2×/muscle/wk.** It barely matters for hypertrophy once weekly volume is equated; it helps strength (≥2 sessions). (Strong hypertrophy / Moderate strength) [1][8]
8. **Effort default 2 RIR (RPE 8)**; 3 RIR for beginners, older adults and heavy barbell compounds. Failure is not required. (Moderate) [1][10]
9. **Manage fatigue:** mesocycle 4–8 wk (default 5 working + 1 deload); deload ~1 wk, volume −30–50%, load mostly kept, or on fatigue signals. (Practitioner) [11]
10. **Minimum effective dose is low.** Always be able to offer a bare-minimum version of the plan. After a missed block shrink the plan, do not drop it. (Moderate) [12][13]
11. **Design top-down** (process below): needs → goal → structure → variables → progression → review.
12. **Individualize dose, complexity and progression speed, not the principles.** Women: same rules; no menstrual-cycle programming. (Moderate) [14][15]

## The fit-between-plan-and-person frame

A plan is judged on six fits. Failing any one fails the plan.

| Fit | Question | Where decided |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Do exercises, reps, load, rest and conditioning match the primary goal? | [goals](04-goals.md) |
| Dose | Is weekly volume inside the range for this experience level and goal? | [variables](03-variables.md) |
| Recovery | Do hard exposures to the same muscle have spacing, and is there a deload? | [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md), [progression](08-progression.md) |
| Constraints | Does it fit the stated days, minutes, equipment, injuries? | [intake](02-intake.md), [exercise library](12-exercise-library.md) |
| Floor | Does it meet (or ramp to) the health floor? | this page |
| Behavior | Will this person do it? (enjoyment, simplicity, history of dropping out) | this page |

## The principles and how each shows up in a plan

| Principle | Definition | Shows up in the plan as | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | Adaptations are specific to action, velocity, ROM, muscle group, energy system, intensity and volume trained. | Heavy ≥80% 1RM, 1–6 reps for max strength; hard sets at any load 30–100% 1RM (practical 6–15 reps, up to 30) for size; 30–70% 1RM with max velocity intent for power; running/cycling for aerobic capacity. | Strong [2][3] |
| **Progressive overload** | No added demand, no further adaptation. | A stated rule per lift: add reps within a range, then load; beginners add load every session. Load goes up when the top of the rep range is hit at target RIR. | Strong [1][2] |
| **SRA (stimulus–recovery–adaptation)** | Training creates fatigue; adaptation happens in recovery; the next stimulus is timed after it. Practitioner framework, not a measured law. | ≈48–72 h between hard sessions for the same muscle; rest days; sleep/stress-aware volume. | Practitioner |
| **Individualization** | Prescription depends on training status, goal, constraints, response. | Beginner = low volume, simple movements, 3 RIR; advanced = more volume, variation, deloads. Constraints drive split and exercise choice. | Moderate [1][2] |
| **Managed variation** | Planned change over time, not random change. | Keep main lifts constant for a whole mesocycle; rotate accessories at block boundaries; change rep/RIR targets by plan. Periodized > non-periodized for 1RM only (ES 0.31); no difference for hypertrophy; the specific model matters less than having progression. | Moderate [16] |
| **Fatigue management** | Accumulated fatigue masks performance and raises overreach/injury risk. | RIR caps (no routine failure), per-session soft cap of 10 fractional hard sets per muscle, spacing, deload, volume cuts of 20–40% when sleep <6 h or stress is high. | Practitioner |
| **Reversibility** | Gains regress after stopping. Strength is retained better and longer than size or strength endurance. | Maintenance and re-entry blocks (see below). | Moderate [17][18] |
| **Minimum effective dose (MED)** | Smallest dose that still produces a meaningful result. | A "bare minimum" version: whole body, 1–3 compounds, 1–3 hard sets each, ≥2 d/wk. | Moderate [12][13] |
| **Adherence / sustainability** | The plan must be done to work. | Feasible days and duration, liked exercises, low friction, explicit fallback week. | Moderate [9] |

## Dose reference (what "enough" means)

All numbers are the binding defaults; detail in [variables](03-variables.md).

| Target | Weekly hard sets/muscle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy, beginner | **8** (6–10) | Practitioner |
| Hypertrophy, intermediate | **12** (10–20) | ACSM 2026 ≥10; Pelland 2025 |
| Hypertrophy, advanced / priority muscle | **16** (12–20; ≤~22 short-term) | Practitioner |
| Max strength (lift-specific) | **6** (4–10): 2–3 sets × 2–3 sessions | 2–3 sets/exercise/session |
| Maintenance | **4–6**, 1–2 sessions/wk, loads stay heavy | Spiering 2021, Bickel 2011 |
| Minimum useful (general fitness) | ≥1 hard set per major pattern, ≥2 d/wk | |

- Count fractionally: direct set = 1, indirect (synergist) = 0.5. [6]
- Per-session soft cap **10 fractional hard sets per muscle** (≈6–8 direct). Past that, add a session. The ~11 fractional / ~2 direct figures from the per-session preprint are points of undetectable further benefit, **not ceilings** (Contested, preprint). [19]
- Weekly volume matters more than how it is split, once frequency ≥2. [1][6][8]
- Do not copy these numbers to obese, sarcopenic or frail clinical populations: ACSM 2026 excluded them from scope. [1]

## Reversibility, maintenance and re-entry

- **Maintenance** (travel, busy block): keep load heavy, cut volume to ≈⅓ (4–6 sets/muscle/wk), 1–2 sessions/wk. Young lifters kept strength and size up to ~32 wk on 1 session/wk, ~1 set/exercise if relative load stayed high. Older adults need ≥2 sessions and 2–3 sets. Bickel 2011: at ⅓ dose older adults kept *strength* but **not** muscle size. (Moderate) [17][20]
- **Layoffs:** strength mostly holds for roughly the first month in younger adults and declines faster in >65 (direction from Bosquet 2013 meta-analysis; "~28 days" and "~2×" are blog-derived, Practitioner). [18][21]
- **"Regain in about half the layoff time"** is a practitioner estimate, not a measured result. Restart at reduced loads and ramp over a few weeks. [21]
- Re-entry loads by layoff length are in [progression](08-progression.md) and [populations](11-populations.md).

## Adherence (what to do about it)

- Autonomous motivation, competence and intrinsic motives predict long-term participation (Moderate, observational-heavy). [9]
- Enjoyment is linked to adherence (Practitioner/Moderate; pooled effect size not verified). Supervision and accountability help in some populations (Contested/limited). [9]
- Resistance-training adherence versus other modes: mixed evidence (Contested). Do not assume lifting plans stick.
- Levers an LLM controls: fewer, shorter sessions for people with a drop-out history; liked exercises (pattern-equivalent swaps); simple repeating structure; clear success criteria; a fallback "minimum" session; a progress log.

## Top-down design process

Run in this order. Each step constrains the next.

1. **Needs analysis.** Collect the [intake](02-intake.md). Screen red flags first. Find constraints and the health-floor gap. [3]
2. **Goal.** One primary, ≤1–2 secondary per block. State a measurable outcome and timeframe. "Primary gets ~60–70% of weekly hard-set volume" is an unsourced heuristic (Practitioner). See [goals](04-goals.md).
3. **Structure.** Days/week → split (full body / upper-lower / PPL by days available) → session length → rest-day placement. Confirm ≥2 strength days and the aerobic floor. See [splits](06-splits.md), [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md).
4. **Variables.** Exercise selection by movement pattern, order (priority / most technical first; power → strength → hypertrophy → isolation → core → conditioning), sets, reps, RIR, rest, tempo. See [variables](03-variables.md), [session design](05-session-design.md).
5. **Progression.** Explicit rule per main lift, mesocycle length, deload. See [progression](08-progression.md).
6. **Conditioning.** Add aerobic work to reach the floor or the goal. See [conditioning](09-conditioning.md), [hybrid](10-hybrid.md).
7. **QC gate.** Run [quality checklist](14-quality-checklist.md); fix failures; only then return the plan.
8. **Review point.** Schedule a reassessment at the end of each mesocycle (4–8 wk): pain, adherence, performance.

## Decision rules

**Floor and goal**
- IF the person is sedentary or restarting THEN start below the floor (2 strength sessions + walking) and build toward 150 min/wk aerobic + ≥2 strength days. [4][5]
- IF goal = general health THEN plan must reach the floor: ≥150 min moderate (or equivalent) + ≥2 whole-body strength days. [4][5]
- IF goal = strength/hypertrophy/sport THEN still check the floor; aerobic dose may be small but not zero unless the user declines. [4][5]
- IF age ≥65 THEN add multicomponent (balance + strength) work so it totals ≥3 d/wk. [4]

**Time and MED**
- IF ≤2 days or ≤30 min/session THEN whole-body sessions, 1–3 compounds, 1–3 hard sets each, ≥2×/wk. [12][13]
- IF break, travel or busy period THEN offer a maintenance block (≈⅓ volume, load heavy, 1–2 sessions/wk). [17][20]
- IF the user missed sessions THEN shrink to the MED version, restart where the last good week was.

**Experience**
- Classify by **speed of progress**, not years: beginner = progress session to session; intermediate = week to week; advanced = month to month or block to block (Practitioner heuristic).
- IF unsure THEN default to intermediate-lite (≈10 sets/muscle/wk, 2–3 RIR) but start beginners on the lower end if they have <6–12 months of training. [1]
- IF beginner THEN 2–3 whole-body days, few simple exercises, linear progression, 8 sets/muscle/wk, 3 RIR.

**Volume and fatigue**
- IF hypertrophy THEN start in the lower-middle of the range; raise volume only when progress stalls and recovery is fine. [6][7]
- IF plateau on a priority lift for 3–4+ weeks with sleep/nutrition consistent THEN check fatigue, volume, technique before changing the program.
- IF poor sleep (<6 h for several nights) or high stress >1 wk THEN cut volume 20–40%, +1–2 RIR, favor stable lifts, consider early deload (Practitioner). [22]

**Adherence**
- IF the user dislikes an exercise THEN swap for a pattern-equivalent one they like.
- IF history of dropping out or low confidence THEN fewer, shorter sessions with explicit success criteria.

**Safety**
- IF red flags in [intake](02-intake.md) THEN refer/clear first; do not prescribe through them.
- IF pain THEN train around, not through. Conservative rule: ≤3/10 during, settled by next day (Practitioner). Refer red flags. No diagnosis or treatment advice.

## Common mistakes

- Optimizing volume and splits for someone who can train only 2 days/wk.
- No progression rule, no deload, no missed-session rule.
- Skipping intake and prescribing generic volume regardless of experience, age or injury.
- Ignoring the health floor: lifting-only plans with no aerobic work, or cardio-only plans with no strength.
- Random variation (new exercises weekly) that makes progress untrackable.
- Starting beginners at advanced volume, complexity or failure training.
- Treating more as always better; sessions of 90+ min for a user with 45.
- Assuming a break resets everything. Plan a ramp-back, not day one.
- Citing experience-based day counts (2–3 / 3–4 / 4–5) as evidence-based targets; they are legacy ACSM 2009 staging resting on thin evidence (3 studies, n=59), so use as Practitioner. [1]
- Treating RP-style landmarks (MV/MEV/MAV/MRV) as validated; they are a heuristic only (Practitioner).

## Evidence notes

- **ACSM 2026** (overview of 137 reviews, >30,000 participants, healthy adults; excludes obesity, sarcopenia, frailty) supersedes the 2009 stand. It finds failure unnecessary, periodization not consistently impactful, load irrelevant for hypertrophy across 30–100% 1RM, and gives **no rest recommendation**. "2–3 RIR" is the authors' suggestion; they say evidence is insufficient to quantify exact RIR targets. [1]
- "Training experience had minimal impact" is the authors' earlier network meta-analysis restated, from mostly novice data. Do not read it as "advanced lifters need the same dose." [1]
- **Pelland 2025** is peer-reviewed (Sports Med). The ACSM "~18–20 sets" diminishing-returns figure comes from it, so they are not independent. The per-session paper is a preprint. [6][19]
- Dose defaults by experience level (8 / 12 / 16) are Practitioner syntheses.
- SRA, MED-per-person and the 60–70% blending heuristic are practitioner frameworks without trials.
- Coleman 2024 (n=39): one week of complete rest did not change hypertrophy; continuous training gave slightly better lower-body strength. Deload timing is therefore a recovery tool, not a proven adaptation booster. [11]
- Minimal-dose data are from trained men (squat/bench) and people not currently training; little data for women, deadlift or elite lifters. [12][13]
- Screening: PAR-Q+ (2023) is the current first-pass screen; the ACSM clearance algorithm here is from secondary guides, so verify against current ACSM guidance before relying on it. [23]
