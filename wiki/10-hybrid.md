# Hybrid / Concurrent Training

> Purpose: combine strength and endurance in one plan: judge interference risk, sequence and space sessions, pick a weekly template, and periodize emphasis.
> Use when: the user wants strength + endurance together (hybrid athlete, HYROX, CrossFit-style, lifter adding cardio, runner adding lifting).
> Depends on: [conditioning](09-conditioning.md) (zones, protocols, dose), [weekly planning](07-weekly-planning.md), [splits](06-splits.md), [progression](08-progression.md), [goals](04-goals.md).

## Key rules (TL;DR)

1. **Interference is small for strength and size, real for explosive strength.** Schumann 2022 (43 studies): max strength SMD −0.06 (95% CI −0.20 to 0.09, n.s.), hypertrophy SMD −0.01 (−0.16 to 0.18), explosive strength SMD −0.28 (−0.48 to −0.08). `Moderate` [1]
2. **Only moderator that mattered:** same-session / <3 h gap vs ≥3 h apart, and **only for explosive strength**. Aerobic type (running vs cycling), frequency, age and **training status did not moderate** (Schumann). `Moderate` [1]
3. **Male lower-body strength is hit more** (Huiberts: males SMD −0.43, females +0.08 with wide CI; group difference P=0.03). Female data are sparse: apply the same rules, do not assume immunity. `Moderate`/`Contested` [2]
4. **Same session, strength priority → lift first** (Murlasits: +3.96 kg lower-body 1RM, CI 0.81–7.10; Eddens: +6.91% lower-body dynamic strength). VO2max effect of order is n.s. `Moderate` [5][6]
5. **Spacing:** ≥6 h apart or separate days; ≥24 h before key quality sessions (Robineau). `Moderate` (single small RCT) [7]
6. **Priority decides the cap:** strength/size priority → 2–3 endurance sessions/wk, mostly easy, ≤1 hard; endurance priority → 2 lift sessions/wk is enough; equal → 2–3 + 2–3 with a hard-easy structure. `Practitioner`
7. **Recovery, not molecular interference, is the practical limiter** (sleep, deficit, stacked high-impact days, abrupt volume jumps). `Practitioner`/`Moderate`
8. **Keep hard runs and heavy leg lifting on different days;** never a leg day immediately before a key run/interval. `Moderate` [7][9]
9. **Default periodization is concurrent** (all qualities weekly); use a 4–8 wk emphasis block (other quality at maintenance, ≥1 session/wk) for peaking or a neglected quality. `Practitioner`
10. **HYROX / CrossFit / hybrid-athlete programs are `Practitioner` evidence:** use as templates, not proof. Mixed-modal sessions are not a substitute for dedicated heavy lifting.
11. Wilson 2012 duration/frequency thresholds ("50–60 vs 20–30 min/day", "≤3 d/wk") are **unverified**: do not state as facts. Direction only: more endurance frequency/duration → more interference. `Moderate` [3]

## Interference evidence

**Definition:** endurance training attenuating strength/mass/power adaptations compared with lifting alone. Original: Hickson 1980 (10 wk; S 5 d/wk, E 6 d/wk, S+E both): S+E strength matched S for ~7 wk then levelled off and declined in weeks 9–10; VO2max rose ~20–25% in E and S+E, not in S. High-frequency design: overreaching is a plausible contributor. `Moderate` (small historical study) [4]

| Source | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Schumann 2022 (43 studies; supervised concurrent ≥4 wk vs identical strength-only) | Max strength −0.06 n.s.; hypertrophy −0.01 n.s.; explosive −0.28 (p=0.007). Attenuation of explosive strength larger same-session (p=0.043) than ≥3 h apart (p>0.05). No moderation by run/cycle, frequency (>5 vs <5/wk), status, age [1] | Moderate |
| Huiberts 2024 (59 studies, n=1346, ages 18–50) | Lower-body strength males −0.43 (−0.64 to −0.22), females 0.08 (−0.34 to 0.49). No sex difference for upper-body strength (P=0.67), power (P=0.37), VO2max (P=0.13); hypertrophy data insufficient. **Untrained** (not trained) had lower VO2max gains with concurrent training (P=0.04). No other training-status effects [2] | Moderate/Contested (sex) |
| Wilson 2012 (21 studies, 422 effect sizes) | Concurrent < strength-only for hypertrophy, strength, power. Running (not cycling) → significant decrements in hypertrophy/strength. Correlations with endurance frequency (r −0.26 to −0.35) and duration (−0.29 to −0.75). Older, correlational [3] | Moderate |
| Sabag 2018 (concurrent HIIT + RT) | Lower-body strength gains smaller vs RT alone; hypertrophy and upper-body strength similar; non-significant trend for cycling HIIT being worse than running HIIT [10] | Moderate |
| Doma 2019 / Wang & Bo 2024 (narrative) | Resistance-induced muscle damage degrades subsequent run quality; spacing and mode/intensity matter [9][11] | Moderate |

**Reconciling Wilson vs Schumann:** magnitude differs by study inclusion and endurance dose; running-vs-cycling is `Contested`: state both (Wilson: running worse; Schumann: no moderation). Do not hard-code "cycling is safer for strength".

**Mechanisms:** AMPK→mTOR suppression is not demonstrated in humans (`Contested`) [12]. Fatigue/overreaching (endurance fatigue lowers lifting quality; lifting damage lowers running quality) is the actionable one. Slow-twitch shift blunting rate of force development is theory (`Practitioner`).

**Asymmetry:** lifting has little effect on endurance outcomes. Untrained people may gain less VO2max when combining (Huiberts, P=0.04); trained/highly trained endurance athletes did not [2].

## Interference dose guide (strength/hypertrophy priority)

`Practitioner` unless graded; thresholds are conservative author defaults, not verified cut-offs.

| Variable | Low risk | Moderate | High risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endurance days/wk | 2 | 3 | 5–6 |
| Easy-session length | 20–30 min | 30–50 min | 50–60+ min daily |
| Hard (threshold/VO2max) sessions/wk | 0–1 | 1–2 | 3+ |
| Gap lifting ↔ endurance, same day | ≥6 h | 3–6 h | 0 h (back-to-back) |
| Gap before a key quality session | 24 h | — | <6 h |
| Weekly run volume, lifting priority | ≤15–25 km (2–3 runs) | | >40 km (unsourced threshold) |

Who should be extra cautious: low recovery capacity (sleep, stress, deficit), people already at the limit of resistance or endurance recovery, power/sprint athletes, trained males prioritising leg strength. `Practitioner` [8]

## Sequencing and spacing

| Priority | Same-session order | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Lower-body strength | Lift → endurance | Moderate [5][6] |
| Aerobic capacity | Little difference; endurance first if run quality (race pace, intervals) is the priority | Moderate (small) [5] |
| Power / speed | Power fresh, first; keep endurance ≥6 h away or on another day | Moderate [1][7] |
| Upper-body lifting + any cardio | Order matters little | Practitioner |

Robineau 2016 (58 rugby players, 7 wk, 2 sessions/wk of each quality, strength always before aerobic): bench and half-squat gains lower at 0 h than at 6 h, 24 h or strength-only; isokinetic MVC at 180°/s likely higher at 24 h and strength-only than at 0 h *and* 6 h; VO2peak gain greater at 24 h than 0/6 h. Authors: avoid <6 h between contradictory qualities. Magnitude-based inference, small trial [7]. Wang & Bo 2024 (narrative): 3–6 h favours muscle fitness, ≥6 h after high-intensity endurance, 24 h favours endurance adaptation; >48 h may help some people (weakly sourced) [11].

Spacing rules:
1. Two-a-day → ≥6 h apart; order per priority (strength first if strength matters).
2. Otherwise separate days.
3. Hard run/intervals ≥24 h from heavy leg lifting (after it) and not the day before a hard leg session either if avoidable.
4. Easy cardio after lifting is low cost.

## Weekly templates

Legend: **S** = strength session, **S-low/S-up** = lower/upper emphasis, **Z2** = easy aerobic (Seiler Z1), **INT** = intervals ([protocols](09-conditioning.md#protocol-library)), **TEMPO** = Z2-zone tempo, **LR** = long easy run/ride, **BR** = HYROX-style brick, **R** = rest. Strength sessions follow [session design](05-session-design.md) and [splits](06-splits.md). Derived from practitioner budgets and adjusted so no INT/LR follows heavy leg lifting within 24 h. `Practitioner`.

### Session budgets

| Days | Strength-primary | Balanced | Endurance-primary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 lift + 1 cardio | 1 lift + 1 INT + 1 LR/Z2 | 1 lift + 2 run (1 INT, 1 long) |
| 4 | 3 lift + 1 Z2/INT | 2 lift + 2 endurance | 1–2 lift + 2–3 run |
| 5 | 3 lift + 2 endurance | 2 lift + 3 endurance | 2 lift + 3 endurance |
| 6 | 4 lift + 2 endurance | 3 lift + 3 endurance | 2 lift + 4 endurance |
| 7 | 4 lift + 2 endurance + easy day | 3 lift + 3 endurance + easy day | 2 lift + 4 endurance + easy day |

3 days cannot maximise both qualities; say so to the user.

### 3 days

| Priority | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | S full (lower-emph) + 10 min Z2 | R | Z2 30–40 (or INT) | R | S full (upper-emph) | R | optional walk |
| Balanced | S full | R | INT run | R | LR or Z2 45 | R | R |
| Endurance | INT run | R | S full (low volume) | R | LR | R | R |

### 4 days

| Priority | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | S-low | S-up | Z2 40 or INT | S full/low | R | optional Z2 | R |
| Balanced | S-low | R | INT run | S-up | R | LR | R |
| Endurance | INT run | S full | R | Z2/TEMPO run | R | LR | R |

### 5 days

| Priority | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | S-up | S-low | Z2 30 (INT optional) | S-up | S-low | Z2 40–45 | R |
| Balanced | INT run | S-low | Z2 30–40 | S-up | R | LR | R |
| Endurance | INT run | S-low (light–moderate) | R | TEMPO run | S-up | LR | R |

### 6 days

| Priority | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | S-low | S-up | Z2 30–40 | S-low | S-up | Z2 45 or INT | R |
| Balanced | INT run | S-low | S-up | Z2 30–40 | S full (light legs) | LR | R |
| Endurance | INT run | S-low (light) + PM Z2 optional | Z2 | TEMPO | S-up | LR | R |

### 7 days (one day must be genuinely easy)

| Priority | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | S-low | S-up | Z2 | S-low | S-up | Z2 or INT | easy walk |
| Balanced | INT run | S-low | S-up | Z2 | S full (light legs) | LR or BR | recovery walk |
| Endurance | INT run | S-low (light) | Z2 | TEMPO | S-up | LR | easy spin |

Template rules:
- INT/LR/TEMPO/BR are "key quality" sessions: max 2 per week (3 only for experienced, high-recovery users); everything else Z1.
- Whichever quality is secondary stays at a maintenance dose (≥1 session/wk) when the week is crowded.
- Strength-primary: keep all lifting volume; cut cardio first (see [conditioning](09-conditioning.md)).
- Endurance-primary: lifting 2×/wk at ~75–85% 1RM, low rep, low fatigue; lifting does not hurt endurance. `Practitioner` [3][8]

## HYROX / CrossFit / hybrid-athlete structure (`Practitioner`)

| Component | Weekly dose | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Runs (easy / tempo / intervals / long) | 2–3 (up to ~30–50 km/wk in advanced plans) | [14][15] |
| Strength | 1–2 (e.g. back squat 4×5 @ 75–80% 1RM, lunges, deadlift, carries) | [14] |
| Race-specific brick / sled / compromised running | 1 (e.g. run–sled–run–wall balls) | [14][18] |
| Run : strength split | 30:70 (strong runner) to 45:55 (intermediate) | [15] |
| Prerequisite | ~30 min continuous running; else 4–8 wk base first | [18] |

- HYROX: running is roughly half of race time (8 km). Plans: 2–3 runs, 1–2 strength, ~1 brick per week [14][15][18].
- **HYROX example (6 days, intermediate):** Mon INT run (e.g. 6×400 m) · Tue S-low (squat 4×5 @ 75–80%) + carries · Wed Z2 · Thu sled push/pull intervals + lunges · Fri easy run or R · Sat brick (run–sled–run–wall balls) · Sun long easy run or R. [14][15]
- Viada-style hybrid athlete: train both qualities together, broad GPP base, narrow to event-specific work in the last few weeks, mostly low-intensity aerobic volume [13][19]. Rogue's reference week stacks lower-body lifting after Z2 the same day and a tempo run into a leg day; this violates the 24 h rule above: treat as advanced/high-recovery only [13].
- CrossFit: constantly varied functional movement at high intensity; traditional 3-on/1-off with a 5-on/2-off alternative; not for beginners initially [16][17]. Use dedicated strength work for heavy loading.
- Nick Bare / Fergus Crawley specifics were not verifiable; do not cite them.

## Periodizing hybrid training

| Situation | Model | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| General fitness / ongoing hybrid | Concurrent: all qualities every week, undulate hard/easy | Practitioner |
| Event in 8–16 weeks | Base/GPP (concurrent) → emphasis block (priority quality ~60–70% of load, other at maintenance 1–2 sessions/wk) → event-specific → taper | Practitioner; block evidence `Moderate`/low quality, endurance-only (20 studies: better VO2max and Pmax vs traditional) [20][21] |
| One quality neglected | 4–8 wk emphasis block, other at maintenance (≥1 session/wk) | Practitioner |

"~60–70% of load to the priority quality" is the same unsourced heuristic as the [goal-blend rule](04-goals.md): a rule of thumb, not evidence. No RCT of block vs concurrent for hybrid athletes exists; no controlled studies in highly trained hybrid athletes.

Mesocycle mechanics (4–8 weeks, volume ramp, deload ~1 wk with 30–50% volume cut, every 4–8 weeks) follow [progression](08-progression.md). Hybrid addition: deload both modalities in the same week.

## Recovery management

- IF sleep <7 h, high life stress, or calorie deficit THEN cut endurance and lift volume 20–30% before cutting frequency; keep intensity. `Practitioner`
- IF main-lift performance drops 2 sessions in a row, or soreness >48 h THEN replace the next hard endurance session with Z2 or rest. `Practitioner`
- IF endurance volume is rising THEN ≤10%/wk (soft ceiling, see [conditioning](09-conditioning.md)) and not in the same week as new heavy leg volume. `Practitioner`
- Deload ~every 4–6 weeks (cut volume 30–50%) or on fatigue signals. `Practitioner`
- Fuel: carbohydrate around long or back-to-back sessions; energy deficit is where interference shows up.
- One genuinely easy or rest day per week at 6–7 days.

## Decision rules

**Priority**
- IF max strength/hypertrophy AND endurance is for health THEN ≤2–3 endurance sessions/wk, mostly easy 20–40 min, ≤1 hard; keep lifting volume intact. [1][3]
- IF power/sprint/jump THEN ≤2 easy endurance sessions, power first and ≥6 h from endurance. [1][7]
- IF endurance event (marathon, HYROX, tri) THEN 2 lifting sessions/wk (~75–85% 1RM, low fatigue); lifting does not hurt endurance. [3][8]
- IF equal hybrid THEN concurrent model: 2–3 lift + 2–3 endurance, hard-easy alternation, ≥1 full rest/easy day.

**Sequencing**
- IF same session AND lower-body strength matters THEN lift first. [5][6]
- IF same session AND endurance quality matters THEN endurance first and keep lifting upper-body or light. [9]
- IF two-a-day possible THEN ≥6 h apart; ELSE separate days. [7][11]
- IF hard run/interval AND heavy leg day THEN separate days, ≥24 h between. [7][9]
- IF upper-body-only lifting THEN same-day cardio is low cost. `Practitioner`

**Modality**
- IF knee/shin/impact tolerance is limited or run frequency >3 d/wk THEN replace some volume with bike/row/ski; evidence does not show cycling clearly protects strength. [1][10]
- IF user wants running performance THEN keep specific runs; not all cross-training.

**Status / sex**
- IF novice THEN interference is small; lifting + easy cardio, add volume slowly (training status did not moderate strength/hypertrophy in Schumann). [1][2]
- IF trained male prioritising leg strength THEN conservative run/bike volume and timing. [2]
- IF female THEN same rules; evidence sparse, no assumed immunity. [2]
- IF untrained AND VO2max is a goal THEN allow for slightly smaller VO2max gains when lifting is added; adding lifting still pays for other outcomes. [2]

## Common mistakes

- Running daily plus lifting 5 d/wk (Hickson-style frequency): overreaching rather than "interference" per se.
- Hard intervals or long runs the day after heavy squats/deadlifts; heavy legs straight after intense running.
- Every endurance session moderate-hard (grey zone); no easy days.
- Treating power athletes like general lifters.
- Abrupt endurance volume jumps.
- Assuming cycling = zero interference or running = always worse.
- Cutting lifting to make room instead of cutting low-value cardio.
- No rest day, no deload, ignoring sleep and fuel.
- Equal emphasis on everything with too few days.
- Using HYROX/CrossFit mixed-modal work as the only strength stimulus.
